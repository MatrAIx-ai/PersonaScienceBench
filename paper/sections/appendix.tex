\subsection{General Principles and Conversion Rubric}
\label{app:conversion-principles}

This subsection expands the conversion and interaction contract summarized
in Algorithm~\ref{alg:benchmark-episode}. The shared rules specify what an
adapter must preserve, what a researcher may know, and when interaction can
change the actor's next action. The following benchmark subsections are
reserved for their specific implementations.

\paragraph{Motivation: make collaboration consequential and observable.}
A solve-and-submit interface can measure the quality of a final scientific
artifact without exposing how its producer negotiates assumptions, obtains
context, or responds to criticism. Adding an optional question channel
alone also permits an episode to finish without consulting the researcher.
Our adaptation therefore gives the researcher an explicit role in reviewing
proposed approaches and reported results. These checkpoints create
opportunities to clarify a consequential choice, request evidence, or
identify a revision before submission. The intended improvement is coverage
of collaborative scientific work, rather than a guaranteed increase in
accuracy or conversation length. A required handoff is a protocol event,
not evidence of spontaneous actor initiative. Additional questions and
revisions must be measured separately. SciConvBench already evaluates
clarification, so it retains its native dialogue instead of receiving
artificial approval interactions.

\paragraph{Preserve scientific identity and solvability.}
Each converted task retains its native identifier, source revision, study
group, scientific objective, required deliverables, and an explicit
evaluation contract. Fixed requirements, including units, methods, output
schemas, thresholds, and data splits, remain unchanged unless the conversion
is declared as a distinct variant. The public brief must expose the basic
scope, permitted resources, access instructions, and submission format.
Researcher-held requirements must be discoverable within the declared
interaction budget. We do not remove arbitrary information or invent
missing facts merely to induce questions. A useful researcher role can
instead consist of discussing choices or reviewing evidence already
accessible to the actor. Shared input data do not by themselves justify
merging distinct questions into one episode.

\paragraph{Allocate information by role.}
Conversion produces separate public inputs $b$, researcher context $k$,
and evaluator configuration $e$, together with interaction protocol $\pi$.
The actor receives the public brief, allowlisted files and tools, actual
runtime information, and shared researcher replies. The researcher receives
permitted task context, its persona, and the shared conversation, including
actor-selected evidence. It does not automatically inspect private actor
reasoning, tool logs, or unshared files. Reference answers, gold solution
artifacts, and hidden tests remain evaluator-only; the controller manages
versions, access, and records. Exporters select explicit fields and files
rather than passing through entire source records or repositories. Access
boundaries must hold in mounts and tool interfaces, not merely in filenames.
Native labels or metadata that are legitimate task inputs may remain
public. Source-context access is distinguished from access to an answer key.

\paragraph{Ground context and distinguish advice from requirements.}
Each extracted fact has a stable identifier, a source revision and path,
and an inspectable locator, such as a field or text span. Facts are typed
as context, fixed requirements, or optional advice; unsupported details
remain unknown. An empty fact pack is valid when the source supplies no
eligible additional context. Automated extraction and validation are
recorded as automated processing rather than human review. Reference
material may be inspected during curation to detect leakage, but it is not
used to synthesize the researcher's runtime knowledge. The full-profile
study permits general scientific knowledge and suggestions in both persona
conditions. Such suggestions do not become new task requirements, and
source-specific assertions still require grounding. A valid fact identifier
does not independently verify every sentence of a generated reply. Claims
about available files, packages, or hardware must match the actual runtime.

\paragraph{Separate researcher identity from the scientific task.}
The persona is a versioned configuration distinct from the task context.
Persona off retains the same common instructions, knowledge permissions,
and interaction protocol with the identity block removed. The actor and
evaluator do not receive that block. Across persona conditions, fixed task
facts, objectives, tools, budgets, and evaluation settings are intended to
remain constant; any departures must be recorded. Actual questions,
disclosures, and review decisions may differ as the conversation develops.
A change to knowledge access or disclosure policy is a separate experimental
factor, not solely a change in communication style. Persona instructions
cannot authorize fabricated evidence or override scientific constraints.
These rules define the comparison; they do not establish that historical
runs with unequal coverage or evaluator versions achieve perfect control.

\paragraph{Require meaningful checkpoints, not extra dialogue.}
For execution and design tracks, the researcher first requests inspection
of the inputs and a proposed approach. The actor's plan handoff reports its
observations, choices, evidence, assumptions, and uncertainties. The
researcher approves the approach or requests a focused clarification.
After approval, the actor performs the analysis or prepares the design and
returns a result handoff with the required deliverable and supporting
evidence. The researcher may request a revision for an identifiable
mismatch or unresolved uncertainty, or conclude the episode. It is
instructed to distinguish actor reports from independently verified
findings and never to act as the final grader. There is no minimum quota of
additional questions and no reward for extending the conversation.

The actor's workspace and both roles' conversation histories persist within
an episode and reset between independent episodes. Tool calls, researcher
questions, shared progress, and review decisions are recorded separately.
Protocol and resource limits are declared per run. In the recorded
\texttt{researcher-review-v2} protocol, approach approval is required before
a result handoff, and researcher-selected stopping is allowed only during
result review. Thus an unresolved input problem can consume the budget in
approach review without producing a submission; this behavior must remain
visible in historical interpretation. A revised stopping rule defines a new
protocol version. SciConvBench instead ends on a completed specification
or its native dialogue limit and does not execute a scientific solver.

\paragraph{Seal deliverables and retain source-specific evaluation.}
The controller checks the submission contract and seals the selected final
artifact before evaluation. A plan, an incomplete handoff, or a budget
exhaustion is not silently promoted to a successful final submission. In
the review protocol, a complete candidate is submitted only after the
researcher selects termination. Public format checks and normal execution
feedback are permitted, but hidden evaluator feedback cannot become an
iterative hint channel. Researcher approval is therefore distinct from
scientific correctness. Execution-based graders remain execution-based;
LLM-scored tracks record their judge, prompt, parser, and aggregation
versions. Converting scores to a common numerical scale does not make their
scientific meanings equivalent.

Declared adaptations are part of this contract. EXP-Bench evaluates proposed
experimental designs rather than executed experiments. ASI-Bench gives the
actor its objective-only request and the researcher eligible guidance from
the next-richer prompt level, creating a collaborative variant of the
original condition. BixBench schema context can also be found in public
inputs, whereas SciConvBench's intended specification supplies researcher-
held context. These differences are retained rather than forcing identical
information asymmetry or deliverable types across benchmarks.

\paragraph{Acceptance rubric and traceability.}
\tabref{tab:conversion-rubric} defines the evidence required to accept a
conversion. It is a construction rubric, not an additional score assigned
to an actor or conversation. Adapters produce a versioned task package or a
recorded exclusion without per-case manual editing. Catalog conversion,
asset readiness, successful execution, and scientific correctness are
separate states. A schema-valid package is not evidence that the source
context is scientifically adequate or that the runtime is ready.

All attempts remain in the record, including missing submissions, timeouts,
and provider or evaluator failures. Any penalty or repair policy must be
stated while preserving the original status; a retry is not an independent
trial or permission to select the best result. Related questions retain
study identities for analysis. Automated checks target concrete failures,
such as leaked answer fields, unknown input roles, invalid artifact mapping,
or missing outcomes disappearing from an aggregate. The rubric specifies
what must be checked; it does not assert that every source case has passed
all checks or is runnable in every environment.

\subsection{DiscoveryBench}
\label{app:discoverybench}
DiscoveryBench~\cite{majumder2024discoverybench} is converted into a
collaborative data-analysis task whose deliverable remains a natural-language
hypothesis supported by the supplied data. The actor receives the original
question, dataset files, and public dataset and column descriptions. The
simulator receives source-provided domain knowledge, separated into
provenance-linked context or advice, but receives neither the reference
hypothesis nor the reference workflow. Missing study details remain
unspecified. Questions from the same study retain their study identifier,
even when they use different metadata records. For test tasks, evaluation
references are retrieved from the released answer key rather than the
placeholder hypothesis fields in task metadata.

The shared review protocol makes the proposed analysis and its reported
evidence available for researcher feedback: the actor first presents its
approach, then analyzes the data and submits its hypothesis for review.
An analysis workflow and supporting code or outputs may accompany the final
hypothesis. Scoring retains the decomposition into scientific context,
variables, and relations: context recall is multiplied by the mean product
of variable F1 and relation agreement. The configured judge is connected to
the evaluator, while reference material remains evaluator-only.
This score measures agreement between the submitted and reference hypotheses;
it does not independently verify that an accompanying analysis was executed
correctly. The conversion therefore records shared evidence and supporting
artifacts separately from hypothesis agreement.

\subsection{BixBench}
\label{app:bixbench}
BixBench~\cite{mitchener2025bixbench} retains the original open-ended question
and its associated data capsule, including any specified variables, methods,
and adjustments. Conversion exports permitted input files from the capsule
and excludes reference notebooks and answer-bearing annotation fields.
Archive and extracted-file hashes identify the inputs used. Unresolved file
roles prevent a partial input inventory from being declared ready. Researcher
context is derived from those inputs, including available column names,
worksheet structure, identifiers, labels, and file-format information.
Automatically extracted schema facts are distinguished from reviewed
annotations. This context is also accessible through the actor's own data
inspection; interaction provides an opportunity to explain or review the
analysis rather than an artificially exclusive route to the schema.
Unspecified code meanings and preprocessing choices are not supplied as facts.

After approach approval, the actor performs the analysis and presents its
answer and evidence for researcher review. A final submission requires both
an explicit declaration that the analysis was executed and an analysis script
or notebook. These requirements form an additional submission gate; they do
not establish that the analysis is scientifically correct. The answer is
then evaluated with the task's string, range, or language-model
verifier against the private ideal answer. The original verifier selection
and answer-parsing rules are retained. Thus, the benchmark score concerns
answer correctness, while the analysis artifact and interaction trace support
separate examination of how that answer was obtained.

\subsection{EXP-Bench}
\label{app:expbench}
EXP-Bench~\cite{kon2025expbench} is adapted as an explicitly design-only task.
The actor receives the scientific question and approved public task material
in a staged workspace, and develops an experimental design with a rationale,
controls, an analysis plan, and feasibility considerations. Researcher context
is selected from source setup passages using cached, automated classification.
The classifier selects exact source spans as background or fixed requirements;
a requirement is eligible only when established by the question. Expected
outcomes, procedural recipes, and experimental choices that the actor is
supposed to make are excluded. The classifier never receives the reference
design. Empty context sets are permitted, and cases with invalid references
or mismatched questions and references are excluded during construction.

The researcher reviews the proposed approach before the actor develops the
full design, then requests revisions or accepts the completed design. The
accepted text is saved as the design checkpoint; neither experimental
execution nor an empirical conclusion is required. Evaluation uses the
released reference-coverage judge, comparing the design with private
reference design elements. Repeated judgments are retained and averaged,
and the released integrity monitor is applied separately to the saved trace.
Both the coverage score and the monitor-gated score are recorded: a rejected
trace receives zero after gating, while transport failures remain evaluation
failures. Invalid monitor output is retried under the released parsing policy
and receives a distinct parse-error status if unresolved. These outputs
measure reference coverage under the design-only adaptation, not successful
execution or reproduction of the source experiment.

\subsection{SciConvBench}
\label{app:sciconvbench}
SciConvBench~\cite{somasekharan2026sciconvbench} already makes clarification
central to the task, so conversion preserves its native dialogue rather than
adding approach and result reviews. The actor receives either an incomplete
scientific request or a request containing inconsistencies. The simulator
holds the corresponding complete or corrected specification and answers
clarification questions under the assigned persona. This specification
represents the researcher's intended requirements, not a solution produced
by a scientific solver. Native issue annotations are retained for evaluation.
Construction requires a valid request, intended specification, and issue
annotations, preserves source case identities, and applies the native
20,000-character request limit without filtering on previous models' success.

The actor can ask up to ten clarification questions and can terminate earlier
by marking its specification complete. If the question limit is reached, the
runner requests a final specification. The specification, conversation,
and intent judges evaluate whether missing information or inconsistencies
were resolved, whether the relevant issues were addressed in dialogue, and
whether the final specification captures the intended task. The implementation
retains recovery before the intent gate as well as the gated outcome, and
separately records clarification precision, clarification recall, and memory
consistency. Keeping specification recovery and conversational grounding
separate distinguishes resolution supported by dialogue from silent
resolution. No scientific solver is executed, so success here concerns
recovering and expressing the intended task rather than solving it.

\subsection{ASI-Bench}
\label{app:asibench}
ASI-Bench~\cite{zhou2026asibench} is instantiated as a declared collaborative
variant: the actor receives the native B3 task, while the simulator can offer
methodological guidance extracted from B2. The public package preserves the
B3 objective, scientific constraints, input data, runtime declarations, and
required output paths. Researcher facts are drawn only from explicitly
identified method, approach, guidance, or algorithm sections of B2, with
passages explicitly identifying reference results or expected answers
excluded. These facts are marked as optional advice and retain their source
locations. The reference directory remains evaluator-only. This condition
must therefore be interpreted as B3 with researcher-held B2 consultation;
it does not measure autonomous performance under the original B3 condition.

The actor submits an approach for approval, executes the scientific task,
and presents its results for researcher review. Final artifacts must use
the exact relative paths required by the native task. Evaluation copies the
sealed submission into a separate workspace and runs the task scorer
with its private reference assets, preserving task-specific gates, weights,
and score limits. Where a task invokes a language-model judge, an adapter
connects the configured judge to the scoring procedure without
replacing its rules. Scorer failures remain distinct from scientific scores.
Runtime availability is recorded during task preparation rather than inferred
from successful conversion, because producing a valid task package does not
establish that its numerical dependencies or required assets are available.
