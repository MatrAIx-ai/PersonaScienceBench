\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

\subsection{Research Questions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Behavioral diversity:} Do profiles induce repeatable interaction patterns beyond ordinary run variation?
  \item \textbf{Performance and fields:} When do profiles help or hinder, and how do effects vary with task format and expertise relationship?
  \item \textbf{Actor comparison (proposed extension):} Do different actors respond differently to the same collaborator panel?
  \item \textbf{Conversation cases:} What evidence links profile wording to researcher behavior, actor decisions, and outcomes?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Models, Conditions, and Repeated Trials}
In the main campaign the actor, the researcher simulator, and the configured
model judge all use \texttt{gpt-6-astra} through the direct OpenAI Responses
API with reasoning effort \texttt{low}. Reasoning effort \texttt{none} was
originally intended but is unsupported by this model, so \texttt{low} was
selected explicitly and held fixed across roles and conditions. Native
non-model evaluators remain in place wherever a source benchmark provides one,
so model judging is used only where the source itself requires it. The earlier
exploratory 30-profile pilot used a different configuration---\texttt{GPT-6
Luna} at reasoning effort \texttt{none}---and its results are therefore
reported as a selection stage rather than pooled with the main campaign.

A trial is one complete pass over the 200-task panel for a set of conditions.
Every task--condition cell receives three independent complete episodes, each
beginning from the original task with a fresh conversation in which both actor
and simulator generate a new trajectory. All three outputs are scored and their
mean and within-condition spread are reported; the best of three is never
selected, and three endings are never generated from one shared conversation.
Within each trial the persona-off baseline is run on the same tasks and its mean
is reused as the paired reference for every persona comparison in that trial.

Campaign scope is reported in phases to keep completed work distinct from
planned work. The initial campaign covered three conditions---persona off,
148, and 155---at $200 \times 3 \times 3 = 1{,}800$ episodes. A subsequent
expansion adds the nine remaining top-deviation profiles at $9 \times 200
\times 3 = 5{,}400$ episodes, reusing the matching persona-off baselines and
the existing 148 episodes rather than regenerating them. The top-ten panel plus
baseline therefore comprises 6,600 episodes, and 7,200 including the retained
155 condition. These are planned totals.

\subsection{Execution and Resource Budgets}
Each trial requests 128\,GB RAM, 30 CPUs, and a 12-hour Slurm allocation, with
up to 30 concurrent episode processes and scientific BLAS/OpenMP thread counts
pinned to one to prevent oversubscription. Protocol limits are twelve
researcher requests and sixty actor calls per episode in the execution and
design tracks, and up to ten question turns plus a final attempt in the
SciConvBench track. Per-role output limits and native grading contracts are
retained, including EXP-Bench's three judge repetitions, which are grading
repetitions rather than independent episode trials.

Expansion batches are submitted as nine sequential 600-episode jobs chained
with \texttt{afterany} dependencies to avoid overlapping API load, grouped as
[046, 003, 004], [089, 001, 127], and [262, 224, 026] and repeated across the
three trials. An \texttt{afterany} dependency enforces non-overlap but does not
certify successful completion, so errors remain visible for later repair rather
than being masked by the scheduler.

Provenance is recorded per trial. Trial~1 processed 600 episodes in
3\,h\,21\,min\,22\,s, yielding 536 scored episodes, 32 missing submissions, and
32 technical errors; three episodes retained from an earlier interrupted
campaign are documented as such. Trials~2 and~3 were submitted fresh with
identical task inputs and settings and reuse no episode results. A previously
cancelled seven-condition job is not counted as an additional trial.

\subsection{Outcome and Interaction Metrics}
Outcome metrics are the native score of each source benchmark, the submission
and completion rates, the paired score change relative to the persona-off
baseline on matched tasks, and the score conditional on submission. Missing
submissions are counted as zero in the all-attempt score and are additionally
reported separately, so that a persona that suppresses submission entirely is
distinguishable from one that produces weak but complete work.

Interaction metrics are recorded separately from outcomes and are not combined
into a composite. Collaboration volume is measured by total exchanges,
additional approach-review rounds beyond the mandatory first, result revisions,
and exhausted dialogue budgets. Actor initiative is measured by questions and
new proposals raised inside actor handoffs, kept distinct from researcher-driven
extra reviews. In the SciConvBench track the native runner
supplies clarification recall (issues asked about over annotated issues),
clarification precision (relevant question turns over all question turns),
conversation-grounded resolution rate, final resolution rate, silent resolution
rate, memory consistency, and intent capture. The conversation-grounded rate is
the source's primary success metric, since it requires that annotated issues were
both resolved and explicitly clarified with the researcher; the silent rate
isolates cases resolved by unstated assumption. Together these distinguish asking
more from asking usefully, and resolving through dialogue from resolving without
grounding. Because the published per-domain decomposition of final resolution
into grounded and silent components does not hold exactly in the source
\citep{somasekharan2026sciconvbench}, each component is reported as exported and
none is reconstructed from the others.
Communication is measured by visible reply characters per role, language choice,
actor language matching, and API token usage, with visible-message length and
token usage treated as distinct quantities and whitespace word counts avoided
as a multilingual comparison.

For each persona--task pair the field relationship is recorded as direct,
adjacent, outside, or unknown, supported by the actual injected profile, with
methodological familiarity such as statistics or coding recorded separately. A
domain label alone does not establish expertise, and this separation is
enforced when interpreting expertise effects.

\subsection{Aggregation and Statistical Analysis}
Analyses use matched task sets: a task is included for a comparison only when
all compared conditions produced an evaluable result, and technical errors are
reported separately from substantive failures such as missing submissions. In
Trial~1 this matched evaluable set retained 185 of 200 tasks---31 ASI, 40 Bix,
34 Discovery, 40 EXP, and 40 SciConv, with super-family counts of 46 life, 57
physical and engineering, 54 mathematics and computer science, and 28 social
sciences and humanities. These are analysis counts for one trial, not
replacement sampling quotas, and later trials may exhibit different failure
patterns.

Independent repeats are averaged within each task--condition cell before
aggregation, and paired baselines are retained rather than collapsed into
marginal means. Super-family summaries are task-weighted. Because multiple
tasks may derive from a single source study, uncertainty estimation must be
source-study aware rather than treating tasks as independent. The
multiple-comparison family is predefined for the expanded panel before
analysis, and interim task-bootstrap results from the three-condition phase are
labeled as interim and are not presented as final repeated-run inference. The
existing three-arm confidence-interval script covers only the initial
conditions and must not be applied as though it covered all ten personas.

\subsection{Optional Extensions}
Two extensions are designed but unexecuted and are reported as proposals, not
contributions. An \emph{actor comparison} would run additional actor models on
the same task and persona panel with simulator, evaluator, tools, and budgets
held fixed, reporting mean and worst-persona performance, completion, and
whether actor ranking changes across collaborators. A \emph{static reference}
would place these results beside conventional non-interactive evaluation on ten
tasks: task-owned context supplied upfront, no simulator, evaluator answers
still private, and comparable actor budgets. The static reference is a
contextual anchor; the primary comparison remains interactive persona-off
versus interactive persona-on.
