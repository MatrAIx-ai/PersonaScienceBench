# Experiment design: directional retrieval of celebrity family relationships

## 1. Requirements, approved choices, and scope

**Source-supported task:** design an experiment testing whether LLMs retrieve parents from children’s names more successfully than children from parents’ names, using real-world celebrity relationships. This is a design-only deliverable. No dataset has been assembled and no model evaluation has been run.

**Approved operational choices, not clarified source intent:**
- Primary endpoint: retrieving **any valid relative** in the requested direction.
- Secondary endpoints: recovery of each sampled parent–child edge, and set precision/recall when requesting all eligible relatives.
- English, closed-book queries in fresh contexts; several pinned model versions.
- Sample adult, publicly documented pairs in which both people satisfy a reproducible prominence rule. Include biological and adoptive relationships with labels; exclude step-only relationships from the main study.

The primary endpoint tests **practical directional retrieval**, not necessarily retrieval of the identical fact. A response naming a sampled parent’s other child can be fully correct. The secondary paired-edge endpoint tests whether the actual sampled relationship is recovered in both directions. Cardinality-matched analyses assess whether unequal numbers of permissible answers change the conclusion.

Generalization is limited to the declared, source-verifiable population, not all celebrities, private families, languages, or model configurations.

## 2. Population and ground-truth construction

### Reproducible eligibility

Proposed implementation assumptions:
1. Freeze a source snapshot, relationship as-of date, and roster before querying models. Illustratively use **1 January 2025** as the as-of date; this is not evidence that every model was trained on all preceding facts.
2. Each endpoint person must have reached age 18 by that date. Deceased people who reached adulthood are eligible.
3. Define public prominence reproducibly: both people have an English Wikipedia biography in the frozen snapshot, a stable identity identifier, and substantive biographical coverage from at least two independent reputable publications dated before the cutoff. A mere passing mention or syndicated duplicate does not qualify. Log the inclusion rubric, source-search protocol, and exclusion reasons.
4. Construct candidate biological/adoptive parent–child links from a frozen structured source such as Wikidata, then verify independently. This creates a reproducible **source-frame population**, not a census of every qualifying celebrity. Report frame omissions and coverage limitations.
5. Exclude step-only, guardian, godparent, and fictional relationships. Step relationships have different social definitions and prominence/exposure patterns; any later study of them should use separate prompts and estimates.

Do not use tested-model outputs to decide who enters the sampling frame. Verify dates and identities, resolve homonyms using IDs, and label biological/adoptive/both/uncertain explicitly. Exclude uncertain edge types from the main roster rather than guessing.

### Ground truth

For every retained identity and queried direction, store:
- Canonical name, verified aliases/stage names, disambiguating biography information, and identity ID.
- Adult status at cutoff, public documentation dates, relationship type, and citations for each eligible relationship.
- A list of publicly documented eligible parents and children, including relatives who **do not** pass the celebrity sampling rule.
- Completeness status for each list, with evidence and reviewer justification.

Use at least two independent sources for each sampled edge, preferably authoritative biographies, direct public records/statements, or reputable reporting. A structured database is a discovery aid, not sufficient verification alone. Two human reviewers verify each sampled edge and review list-completeness judgments; disagreements go to a third reviewer.

Completeness is a stronger claim than verifying one edge. Mark a list as complete only when explicit family enumeration or comparably strong corroborating documentation supports it. Otherwise mark it incomplete/uncertain. A frozen web page failing to mention a child is not proof that no other child exists.

For answers not already in the table, search according to the same prespecified protocol and admit any answer verified from pre-cutoff public sources. Log such additions without changing the sampled edges. Publicly unknown relationships are outside scope. Do not solicit or infer undisclosed family information.

## 3. Sampling, repeated cues, and the estimand

### Family-first sampling

Define a family as a connected component of the verified biological/adoptive parent–child identity graph used in constructing the frame. Include known linking identities, even when they are not eligible sampled celebrities, so siblings and multigeneration relationships are not treated as independent families. Freeze component assignments before sampling. Report component sizes; a very large dynasty remains one sampling unit.

Proposed main sample: simple random sampling of **300 family components**, followed by simple random selection of up to **two eligible edges per family**. If there are fewer than 300 eligible families, evaluate feasibility before claiming the proposed precision. Choosing only two edges limits cost and prevents large families from dominating. Sampling more edges within a family improves coverage but does not create additional independent families.

Record domain, generation, prominence difference, relationship type, and answer-cardinality distributions for both the frame and sample. The primary sample is not artificially balanced on these characteristics; matching and stratification are prespecified secondary analyses. If later sampling requires disproportionate stratification, revise and register inclusion-probability weights before evaluation.

### Deduplicate queries, not sampled facts

For a given model, template, endpoint, and requested direction, query each unique cue identity only once. A parent appearing on two sampled edges receives one child-retrieval query, not two independent chances. Reuse its response for the relevant edge records. Likewise, reuse child cues with multiple sampled parents. Evaluate the primary and list tasks in separate fresh contexts.

### Estimand

Let edge e in family f join parent p and child c. Let A(c→parent) and A(p→child) be primary correctness indicators. For each pinned model:
1. Average each score over the prespecified templates.
2. Compute the paired difference for each sampled edge: child-to-parent minus parent-to-child.
3. Average edge differences within family.
4. Average family differences with equal family weights.

The population target is the mean directional difference obtained by choosing a family uniformly from the eligible frame and then an eligible edge uniformly within that family. It is **family-balanced and edge-paired**, not a uniform-person or uniform-query estimand. Repeated cues can contribute through multiple edges because that is part of this target, but they are not independent observations. Uniform within-family edge sampling estimates the family’s edge mean; it does not require weighting each sampled family by its number of edges.

Compute directional accuracies using the same weights, so their difference equals the paired contrast. As a sensitivity analysis, report a cue-balanced contrast that weights unique cues equally within each family and direction. Clearly label this as a different estimand. Also report the actual number of unique cue queries.

## 4. Models, prompts, and execution controls

### Proposed configuration

Use three accessible, pinned models from different model families/providers, chosen and registered before results are available. Record exact snapshot IDs, release dates, access dates, system prompts, sampling parameters, and available knowledge-cutoff information. Do not pool them as if they were a random sample of all LLMs.

Use two mirrored wording templates per endpoint, one completion per template at temperature zero or the lowest supported setting. Pin a seed when supported; deterministic settings are not a guarantee of reproducibility. No browsing, retrieval augmentation, tools, demonstrations containing study facts, conversation history, or feedback. Templates are robustness checks, not additional independent families.

Randomize/interleave query order across directions and families. Use identical output limits within each task: illustratively 128 tokens for one-name answers and 1,024 tokens for lists. Register these before querying. Record truncation and malformed responses. Retry transport failures with the identical request; do not retry factual failures. If a pinned version becomes unavailable mid-run, do not silently mix its replacement into the same estimate.

### Common system instruction

“Answer factual questions from your existing knowledge only. Do not use external tools. Follow the requested scope and output format. If you do not know, say UNKNOWN.”

### Primary template A

Child cue:
“As of 1 January 2025, name one publicly documented biological or adoptive parent of {CHILD_NAME}. The person you name must have reached age 18 by that date; deceased people who reached adulthood count. Give one name only, or UNKNOWN.”

Parent cue:
“As of 1 January 2025, name one publicly documented biological or adoptive child of {PARENT_NAME}. The person you name must have reached age 18 by that date; deceased people who reached adulthood count. Give one name only, or UNKNOWN.”

### Secondary template A

Use the same wording and scope, replacing “name one” with “list all” and the final sentence with:
“Give a JSON array of names only, or UNKNOWN if you cannot answer.”

Change parent/parents and child/children grammatically as needed, without adding target-revealing clues.

### Template B

Mirror these two questions exactly except for the relationship word:
“Who is one biological or adoptive {parent/child} of {NAME}, publicly documented by 1 January 2025, who had reached adulthood by that date? Deceased adults count. Return a single name, or UNKNOWN.”

For lists:
“Who are all the biological or adoptive {parents/children} of {NAME}, publicly documented by 1 January 2025, who had reached adulthood by that date? Deceased adults count. Return a JSON array of names, or UNKNOWN.”

Prefer established, unambiguous full/stage names. Where needed, use the same predeclared neutral disambiguation format, such as birth year, in both directions. Never add the relative’s identity or a clue specific to the target edge. Report disambiguated cases separately.

## 5. Scoring and adjudication

### Primary: any-valid-relative accuracy

A response is correct if the first identifiable candidate denotes a source-verified person satisfying **the actual prompt’s relationship, adulthood, and date scope**. It need not be the sampled counterpart or pass the celebrity prominence rule.

Accept verified aliases, stage names, transliterations, and minor spelling errors that identify one person unambiguously. Do not accept a surname shared by multiple plausible people unless the response disambiguates it. Score UNKNOWN, abstention, empty answers, wrong relationships, and unsupported answers as failures; report categories separately.

If a model supplies multiple candidates despite the instruction, score the first identifiable named candidate only and flag the format violation. This prevents a list of guesses from receiving automatic any-hit credit. A relationship asserted in prose can be scored if its candidate is unambiguous. Apply extraction rules uniformly and preserve raw responses.

### Secondary: paired-edge recovery

For each sampled edge, score whether the list requested from the child contains the sampled parent, and whether the list requested from the parent contains the sampled child. Use the same family-balanced paired contrast.

This endpoint concerns the **same verified edge** in both directions, although it uses a different elicitation task from the primary endpoint. Edge recovery alone rewards long lists, so interpret it jointly with precision and false additions, never in isolation.

### Secondary: set performance

Normalize a response to unique identities. For complete reference lists, calculate precision, recall, and F1 against all eligible relatives—not just the celebrity roster. Count names claimed as relatives but unverifiable under the adjudication protocol as unsupported false additions in the strict score. Deduplicate repeated names. Define all three scores as zero for an empty/UNKNOWN response when a verified eligible relative exists, as it does for every sampled cue.

Evaluate scores per cue and map them to edge/family averages for the principal weighted comparison. Also show unique-cue summaries and exact-set accuracy descriptively. Avoid micro-averaging that lets prolific families dominate.

### Incomplete lists and uncertain answers

- Keep verified sampled edges in primary and paired-edge analyses even when the full relative list is incomplete.
- Restrict confirmatory set recall/F1 and cardinality matching to **pairs with sufficiently complete lists on both sides**; report exclusions and compare their prominence/family characteristics with the full sample.
- For incomplete lists, optionally report recall against known relatives as “known-list coverage,” not full recall.
- Distinguish refuted answers from unresolved ones. Report strict accuracy/precision plus an upper-bound sensitivity treating unresolved candidates as valid. A conclusion that changes under these bounds is not robust.
- If a supposedly complete list gains a verified omitted relative during adjudication, amend and version the truth table for every model, recheck completeness, and rescore consistently. Do not privilege the model that suggested it.

Blind answer adjudicators to model identity and aggregate directional results. They must see the requested relationship to judge validity, so full blinding to direction is not possible. Two reviewers independently adjudicate all unresolved/nontrivial answers; audit a random sample of automatic alias matches and report agreement and disagreements.

## 6. Prominence, exposure, and answer-cardinality controls

### Prespecified measurements

Collect only pre-cutoff proxies with documented windows:
- Log English Wikipedia pageviews over the preceding 12 months, with missingness retained explicitly.
- English biography length and language-edition count as alternative prominence measures.
- Age, generation, public domain/occupation, relationship type, and age/date at which the link became publicly documented when available.
- If a fixed suitable corpus is available, person mention counts, pair co-mentions, and counts of parent-oriented versus child-oriented relationship statements.

No co-mention corpus is currently supplied; such analysis is optional, not a prerequisite silently assumed to exist. Do not describe these measures as actual training exposure.

### Prominence analyses

Report the signed child-minus-parent log-pageview difference. Use prespecified groups: child has at least twice the parent’s pageviews; neither has twice the other’s; parent has at least twice the child’s. Define rules for zeros and missing windows before analysis, and report missing-proxy cases separately.

Estimate paired gaps within these groups and in the similar-prominence subset. Examine both cue and target prominence: a directional contrast changes which person serves each role. A prespecified secondary model can relate edge differences to signed prominence difference, overall prominence, generation, and family size, with family-level uncertainty. Keep this model parsimonious and secondary to the unadjusted paired estimates. Do not drop hard cases based on observed model performance.

### Cardinality analyses

For each edge define K_CP as the number of adult publicly documented biological/adoptive parents of the child, and K_PC as the corresponding number of children of the parent. Count **all prompt-valid answers**, not just celebrities.

Report primary and edge-recovery gaps by cardinality and by whether K_CP equals K_PC. Prespecified restricted comparisons:
1. Exact cardinality match: K_CP = K_PC.
2. Singleton/singleton, if sufficiently populated.
3. Joint cardinality match and similar prominence.

A singleton-only restriction may disproportionately select incomplete records or unusual families; require complete lists and report its composition rather than presenting it as automatically representative. Sparse subsets get wide intervals and descriptive interpretation, not forced matching. These restrictions change the target population and do not replace the full-population primary estimate.

## 7. Statistical analysis and interpretation

For each model report weighted accuracy in each direction, the primary paired gap in percentage points, and family-clustered uncertainty. Resample whole sampled families, retaining all sampled edges, shared responses, templates, and endpoints together. A family bootstrap, for example 5,000 replicates, estimates uncertainty over sampled families conditional on the fixed models/templates. With a simple random sample and a small sampling fraction, treating sampled families as independent is a practical conservative approximation to finite-population sampling.

Use two-sided intervals so reversed effects remain visible. The confirmatory hypothesis is positive primary gap for each of three models; control multiplicity across those three tests with Holm’s procedure. Secondary endpoint, template, prominence, and cardinality analyses are prespecified interpretive checks, not extra opportunities to declare the hypothesis supported. Report their intervals and sample sizes without claiming independent confirmations. Give each model’s result rather than only a pooled average.

Prespecify a practical directional effect of **10 percentage points** as an illustrative decision threshold. Also prespecify an **80% child-to-parent accuracy benchmark** if the intended wording includes “accurately retrieving.” These are design assumptions, not source-established standards. Report continuous estimates and uncertainty so other thresholds remain assessable. Higher relative accuracy is not equivalent to high absolute accuracy: 35% versus 20% supports a directional gap but not the illustrative 80% benchmark. Support for exceeding that benchmark should require the corresponding lower confidence bound to exceed it, with multiplicity addressed if used as a formal claim.

Interpretation:
- **Positive primary gap:** evidence of practical directional asymmetry for the sampled population and models. If paired-edge recovery and matched analyses agree, the interpretation is stronger than an any-relative result alone. If the gap disappears or reverses there, emphasize ambiguity, family composition, or exposure-associated differences rather than identical-fact asymmetry.
- **Near-zero gap:** absence of a detected difference is not equivalence. Equivalence within ±10 points requires an appropriately prespecified equivalence analysis and sufficiently narrow uncertainty; otherwise call the result inconclusive at that scale.
- **Negative gap:** evidence against the proposed direction under this setup; inspect the same prespecified controls rather than changing the hypothesis post hoc.
- **Mixed model/template results:** report heterogeneity and restrict conclusions accordingly.

No observational adjustment establishes an intrinsic causal retrieval mechanism, rules out asymmetric training text, or shows the same effect in other populations.

## 8. Illustrative precision and resource plan

The following are hypothetical planning calculations, not observed quantities.

For one family-weighted paired difference D_f in [−1, 1], assume independent sampled families and variance about **0.30**. This resembles a one-edge paired binary comparison with roughly 30% discordant outcomes and a small mean gap; actual variance after averaging edges/templates may differ.

With 300 families:
- SE ≈ sqrt(0.30/300) = 0.0316.
- An unadjusted 95% interval has approximate half-width 1.96 × 0.0316 = **6.2 percentage points**.
- A normal approximation for 80% power at two-sided 5% gives a detectable gap about (1.96 + 0.84) × 0.0316 = **8.9 points**.
- For a 10-point gap, the same approximation gives about **236 families** before multiplicity adjustment. A conservative Bonferroni planning approximation for three models raises that to about **315 families**; 300 is therefore a plausible precision target, not a guaranteed 80%-power claim for all confirmatory tests.

Under the worst-case variance bound of 1, 300 families instead gives approximately **±11.3 points** for an unadjusted 95% interval. Additional edges/templates must not be counted as additional independent families. Subset analyses will have less precision. The final registration should either retain a fixed family count with these limitations or justify a larger count using independent planning information—not optional stopping on observed significance.

At at most 600 sampled edges, there are at most 1,200 directional cue queries per endpoint/template/model before deduplication. Three models × two templates × two endpoints gives at most **14,400 model calls**. Ground-truth verification, especially complete family lists, is likely the main resource bottleneck. Cache sources and outputs, deduplicate cues, and preflight parsing and prompt symmetry on synthetic or disjoint identities without changing scoring based on evaluated outcomes.

**Modest-resource fallback:** 100 families, one edge per family, two pinned models, one template, both endpoints: at most **800 calls** before deduplication. Under the same hypothetical variance of 0.30, an unadjusted 95% half-width is approximately **10.7 points**. Treat this as a coarse estimation study with limited subgroup and equivalence claims, not as a substitute for the proposed precision target. Fix the fallback before any model evaluation.

The eventual study should release, where licensing permits: the versioned source frame and sampling seed; inclusion/exclusion audit; citation-backed relationship table and completeness flags; family components; prominence/cardinality metadata; pinned model configurations; exact prompts; raw responses; adjudication log; and family-level analysis code.

Before evaluation, check identity/alias collisions, relationship labels, adulthood/date eligibility, duplicate cues, family weights summing correctly, mirrored prompt differences, source completeness, and inclusion of valid non-celebrity relatives. Unit-test scoring on fabricated outputs: a correct unsampled sibling, an alias, a wrong stepchild, multiple primary candidates, an ambiguous surname, UNKNOWN, duplicate list entries, and a list containing both valid and invalid people.

The current workspace supports design preparation and local consistency checks only. Data collection needs later source access; evaluation needs access to the selected pinned models. Neither is claimed to have occurred.
