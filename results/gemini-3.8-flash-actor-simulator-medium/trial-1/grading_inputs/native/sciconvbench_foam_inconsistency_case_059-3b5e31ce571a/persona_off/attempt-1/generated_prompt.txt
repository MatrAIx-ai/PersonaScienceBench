Simulate steady-state two-phase oil-water flow in a horizontal pipe using the OpenFOAM solver twoPhaseEulerFoam.
The pipe geometry has an inner diameter of 0.05 meters and an axial length of 2.0 meters.
The continuous phase is water with a density of 1000 kilograms per cubic meter and a kinematic viscosity of 1.0e-6 square meters per second.
The dispersed phase is oil with a density of 880 kilograms per cubic meter and a kinematic viscosity of 2.0e-5 square meters per second.
The interfacial surface tension between water and oil is set to 0.035 Newtons per meter.
The inlet boundary has a uniform axial mixture velocity of 1.0 meter per second.
The inlet boundary has a fixed water volume fraction alpha.water equal to 0.3.
The outlet boundary has a zeroGradient condition for alpha.water.
The outlet boundary has a fixed value of uniform zero Pascals for p_rgh.
The outlet boundary has an inletOutlet condition for phase velocities with an inletValue of zero.
The pipe walls apply a noSlip condition for the phase velocities.
The pipe walls apply a zeroGradient condition for alpha.water.
The pipe walls apply a fixedFluxPressure condition for p_rgh.
Gravity is set to 9.81 meters per second squared acting perpendicular to the pipe axis in the negative vertical direction.
Turbulence is modeled using a standard two-phase k-epsilon turbulence model with standard wall functions on the pipe walls.
Discretize the fluid domain using a structured hexahedral mesh with refinement near the wall boundary.
Run the simulation until the velocity profiles, phase distribution, and pressure drop achieve steady-state convergence.
