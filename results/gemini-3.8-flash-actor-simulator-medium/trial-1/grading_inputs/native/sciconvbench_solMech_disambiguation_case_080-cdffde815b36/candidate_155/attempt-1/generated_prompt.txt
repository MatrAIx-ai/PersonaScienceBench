Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, isotropic, and homogeneous material behavior throughout the shaft.
Model the shaft as a uniform solid circular cylinder with length L and constant diameter d.
Apply Saint-Venant torsion theory where plane circular cross-sections remain plane and do not warp.
Neglect torsional twist induced by transverse shear loading because the shear center coincides with the centroid for circular cross-sections.
Define the polar moment of inertia J as pi times d to the fourth power divided by 32.
Express the angle of twist theta under applied torque T as T multiplied by L divided by the product of the shear modulus G and polar moment of inertia J.
Substitute the polar moment of inertia into the twist relation to yield theta equal to 32 multiplied by T and L divided by pi, G, and d to the fourth power.
Constrain the angle of twist such that theta is less than or equal to the specified allowable angle of twist.
Compute the minimum required shaft diameter d as the fourth root of 32 multiplied by T and L divided by the product of pi, G, and the allowable angle of twist.
Calculate the maximum torsional shear stress at the outer surface as 16 multiplied by T divided by pi times d to the third power.
Calculate the maximum transverse shear stress from the shear load V across the circular cross-section as 4 multiplied by V divided by 3 times the cross-sectional area.
Calculate bending stresses along the length resulting from the transverse shear loading using elementary beam theory.
Combine the normal bending stresses and shear stresses using the von Mises yield criterion or maximum shear stress criterion to verify structural integrity.
Select the final shaft diameter as the maximum of the diameter required by the angle-of-twist limit and the diameter required by strength limits.
