The simulation domain is a 2D rectangular plate spanning from x = 0 to x = 1 m and y = 0 to y = 0.5 m with unit thickness under plane stress assumptions.
A circular hole of radius 0.1 m centered at x = 0.5 m, y = 0.25 m is removed from the domain, with no other cuts or notches present.
The computational mesh is generated with a characteristic resolution corresponding to 64 elements along each edge.
The material follows isotropic small-strain linear elasticity with Young modulus E = 70 GPa, Poisson ratio nu = 0.33, and mass density rho = 2700 kg/m^3.
The reduced plane-stress constitutive law is used to relate the in-plane linear strain tensor to the Cauchy stress tensor.
Body forces represent downward gravitational acceleration with magnitude g = 9.81 m/s^2, yielding the body force vector f = (0, -rho * g).
Homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed on the entire left boundary at x = 0 m, fixing both displacement components to zero.
The boundary of the central circular hole and the remaining unloaded outer boundaries are treated as traction-free.
A rotational torque of magnitude M = 800 N*m is applied along the outer boundary segment within radius r = 0.02 m from the point (1.0, 0.25) m.
The applied traction vector on this boundary region is defined by t = (M / (2 * pi * r)) * e_theta with local tangent vector e_theta = ( -(y - 0.25) / r, (x - 1.0) / r ).
The variational problem is formulated in FEniCS legacy (dolfin) using a vector-valued Lagrange displacement function space.
The resulting linear algebraic system is solved using a direct linear solver.
The von Mises equivalent stress is computed across the domain from the resulting in-plane Cauchy stress tensor components.
