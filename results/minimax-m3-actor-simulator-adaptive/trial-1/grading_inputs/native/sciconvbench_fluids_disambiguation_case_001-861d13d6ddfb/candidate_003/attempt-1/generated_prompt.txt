Calculate the single-phase pressure drop for water flowing through a horizontal straight pipe using the Darcy–Weisbach equation.
Use water properties at 20 °C: density ρ = 998 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity μ = 1.002 × 10⁻³ Pa·s, kinematic viscosity ν = 1.004 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s.
Pipe geometry: inner diameter D = 50 mm (0.05 m), length L = 100 m, horizontal orientation (no elevation change).
Pipe material: galvanized steel with absolute roughness ε = 0.15 mm (0.00015 m).
Volumetric flow rate: Q = 2 L/s = 0.002 m³/s.
Compute the pipe cross-sectional area A = π D² / 4 and the average velocity V = Q / A.
Compute the Reynolds number Re = V D / ν to confirm the flow regime.
Compute the relative roughness ε / D.
Compute the Darcy friction factor f using the Swamee–Jain explicit approximation of the Colebrook–White equation, which is valid across laminar, transitional, and turbulent regimes (or fall back to the laminar formula f = 64 / Re if Re < 2300, and to the Swamee–Jain formula otherwise).
Compute the pressure drop ΔP = f (L / D) (ρ V² / 2).
Report the final pressure drop in pascals (Pa), and also provide it in bar and in meters of water column (mH₂O) for convenience.
Briefly state intermediate results (velocity, Reynolds number, relative roughness, friction factor) alongside the final pressure drop so the calculation is fully verifiable.
