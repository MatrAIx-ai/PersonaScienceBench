Calculate the pressure drop in a horizontal pipe.
The pipe is 100 m long with an inner diameter of 50 mm (0.05 m).
The water flow rate is 2 L/s (0.002 m³/s).
Water properties: density ρ = 998 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity μ = 1 × 10⁻³ Pa·s, temperature = 20 °C.
Pipe material is galvanized iron with absolute roughness ε = 0.15 mm.
Assume the pipe is straight with no fittings, so include only major (frictional) losses and neglect minor losses.
Calculate the mean flow velocity as v = Q / A = 0.002 / (π/4 × 0.05²).
Compute the Reynolds number Re = ρ v D / μ to confirm turbulent flow.
Use the Colebrook–White equation with ε/D = 0.003 and the calculated Re to determine the Darcy friction factor f.
Apply the Darcy–Weisbach equation ΔP = f · (L/D) · (ρ v² / 2) to obtain the pressure drop in Pascals.
Report the final pressure drop in Pa (and optionally in kPa or bar) along with the intermediate results: velocity, Reynolds number, relative roughness, friction factor, and the formula used.
