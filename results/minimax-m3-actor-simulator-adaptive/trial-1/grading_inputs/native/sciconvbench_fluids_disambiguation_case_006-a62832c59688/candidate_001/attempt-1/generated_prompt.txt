The flow is steady, incompressible, and fully-developed between two infinite parallel plates.
The plate separation distance is h = 10 mm (0.01 m), with the y-coordinate measured from the bottom plate.
The bottom plate located at y = 0 is stationary, giving the boundary condition u(0) = 0.
The top plate located at y = h = 0.01 m moves in the x-direction at U = 5 m/s, giving u(h) = 5 m/s.
There is no applied pressure gradient along the flow direction, so dp/dx = 0.
The flow is driven purely by the motion of the top plate (classic plane Couette flow).
The Navier-Stokes equation reduces to μ(d²u/dy²) = 0, since the inertial and pressure terms vanish for this fully-developed flow.
Integrate the governing equation twice to obtain the general solution u(y) = C₁y + C₂.
Apply the no-slip condition u(0) = 0 to determine C₂ = 0.
Apply the no-slip condition u(h) = 5 m/s to determine C₁ = U/h = 5 / 0.01 = 500 s⁻¹.
The final steady velocity profile is u(y) = 500y m/s, with y expressed in meters.
The velocity varies linearly from 0 at the stationary bottom plate to 5 m/s at the moving top plate.
The corresponding constant shear stress throughout the gap is τ = μ(du/dy) = 500μ Pa, where μ is the dynamic viscosity of the fluid.
