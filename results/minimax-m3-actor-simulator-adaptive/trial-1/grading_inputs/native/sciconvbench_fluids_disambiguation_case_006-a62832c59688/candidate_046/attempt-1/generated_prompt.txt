Solve for the steady velocity profile u(y) in Couette flow between two parallel plates.
The lower plate is stationary, located at y = 0, with velocity u(0) = 0.
The upper plate is located at y = h = 0.01 m and moves at a velocity U = 5 m/s in the x-direction, with boundary condition u(h) = 5 m/s.
The separation between the plates is h = 10 mm.
Apply the steady-state incompressible Navier-Stokes equation in the x-direction.
Assume fully-developed, unidirectional flow so that the velocity has the form u(y) only, with v = 0 and w = 0.
Assume the pressure gradient dp/dx = 0 (pure shear-driven Couette flow).
Assume a Newtonian fluid with constant viscosity μ.
Reduce the momentum equation to μ * d²u/dy² = dp/dx under laminar flow conditions.
Integrate the reduced equation twice with respect to y to obtain the general solution u(y) = (1/(2μ)) * (dp/dx) * y² + C₁ * y + C₂.
Apply the no-slip boundary conditions u(0) = 0 and u(h) = 5 m/s to determine the integration constants.
With dp/dx = 0, the constants reduce to C₂ = 0 and C₁ = U/h = 500 s⁻¹.
Write the resulting velocity profile as u(y) = (U/h) * y.
State the final result as u(y) = 500 * y, with y in meters and u(y) in m/s.
Verify that the shear stress in the fluid is constant, τ = μ * (U/h), independent of y.
Present the step-by-step derivation of the Navier-Stokes reduction, integration, application of boundary conditions, and the final expression for u(y).
