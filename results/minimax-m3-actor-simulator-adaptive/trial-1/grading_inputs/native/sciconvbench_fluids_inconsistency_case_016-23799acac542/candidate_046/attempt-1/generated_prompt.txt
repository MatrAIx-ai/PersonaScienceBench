Derive the pressure distribution of a rotating fluid inside a centrifuge using the radial equilibrium equation dP/dr = ρ·r·ω², which states that the radial pressure gradient balances the centrifugal body force per unit volume.
Integrate both sides of dP/dr = ρ·r·ω² with respect to r to obtain P(r) = (ρ·ω²·r²)/2 + C, where C is the integration constant.
Apply the boundary condition P(r = 0) = P₀ to fix the reference pressure at the rotation axis, which gives C = P₀.
Write the final pressure distribution as P(r) = P₀ + (1/2)·ρ·ω²·r², showing the characteristic parabolic increase with radius.
Convert the rotational speed from 10,000 RPM to angular velocity in rad/s using ω = 2π·N/60, yielding ω = 2π·10,000/60 ≈ 1047.2 rad/s, and note that ω² ≈ 1.097×10⁶ rad²/s².
Use the density of water, ρ = 1000 kg/m³, as the working fluid inside the centrifuge.
Define the outer radius of the centrifuge container as R = 0.15 m, so that the pressure at the wall can be evaluated as P(R).
Substitute the numerical values into the symbolic expression to obtain the explicit formula P(r) ≈ P₀ + 5.48×10⁸·r² [Pa], with r in meters.
Evaluate the gauge pressure rise at the outer wall r = R = 0.15 m, giving ΔP = (1/2)·1000·(1047.2)²·(0.15)² ≈ 1.23×10⁷ Pa ≈ 12.3 MPa ≈ 1780 psi above the reference P₀.
Describe the result qualitatively as a parabolic pressure profile with its minimum at the rotation axis (r = 0) and its maximum at the container wall (r = R = 0.15 m).
Note that at 10,000 RPM the gravitational hydrostatic term ρ·g·h is many orders of magnitude smaller than the centrifugal term and can be neglected, so the original P = ρ·g·h formula does not apply.
