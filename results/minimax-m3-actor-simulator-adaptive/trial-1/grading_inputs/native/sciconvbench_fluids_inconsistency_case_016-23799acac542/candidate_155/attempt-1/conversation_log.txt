Q: The formula $P = \rho g h$ applies to static fluids. In a rotating centrifuge, the dominant force is actually centrifugal, which depends on radius $r$ and angular velocity $\omega$. 

Which acceleration should be used to calculate the pressure distribution: standard Earth's gravity ($g$), or the centrifuge's radial acceleration ($\omega^2 r$)?
A: Use the centrifuge's radial acceleration, not standard gravity. For a rotating fluid, the equilibrium equation is:

$$\frac{dP}{dr} = \rho \, r \, \omega^2$$

Integrating this (assuming $\rho$ and $\omega$ are constant) from some reference radius $r_0$ to an outer radius $r$ gives the pressure distribution:

$$P(r) = P(r_0) + \tfrac{1}{2} \rho \, \omega^2 \left(r^2 - r_0^2\right)$$

So please use this rotating-fluid equilibrium approach — the static hydrostatic formula $P = \rho g h$ does not apply here because gravity is negligible compared to the centrifugal effect at 10,000 RPM.
