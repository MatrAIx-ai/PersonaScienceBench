Q: Which working fluid should I assume for the Rayleigh-Pitot calculation at M = 0.5 — standard air with γ = 1.4, or a different gas with a specified ratio of specific heats?
A: Actually, there's an important correction to make here. The Rayleigh-Pitot formula doesn't apply to M = 0.5 because it's specifically for **supersonic** flow with a detached shock wave in front of the Pitot tube. At M = 0.5, the flow is subsonic, so no shock forms and Rayleigh-Pitot isn't the right tool.

For this problem, you should apply the **isentropic flow relations (Saint-Venant)** instead. 

As for the working fluid, the task doesn't explicitly specify a gas, so please use **standard air with γ = 1.4** — that's the standard assumption for Saint-Venant isentropic relations when no other gas is given.

At M = 0.5 in standard air, you should compute (at minimum):
- p/p₀ (stagnation pressure ratio)
- T/T₀ (stagnation temperature ratio)
- ρ/ρ₀ (stagnation density ratio)
