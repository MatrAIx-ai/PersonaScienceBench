Q: There is a contradiction in the request: inviscid flow implies zero viscous friction and therefore zero pressure drop, yet the Darcy–Weisbach equation with f=0.03 will produce a nonzero pressure drop.

Which assumption should I follow for the calculation?
A: Quick answer: drop the "inviscid" wording — treat the flow as **viscous** and use Darcy–Weisbach with a friction factor consistent with the Reynolds number (don't just plug in f=0.03).

Specifically:

- **Assumption to use:** viscous (real) flow. The inviscid bit is inconsistent with computing a friction pressure drop — ignore it.
- **Formula:** Δp = f · (L/D) · (ρ v² / 2)
- **Inputs you have:** L = 25 m, D = 0.080 m, v = 1.5 m/s.
- **Inputs you need to assume/justify (not in the prompt):**
  - Oil density ρ — typical value ~880 kg/m³ (light mineral oil). Use 880 unless your textbook specifies otherwise.
  - Oil kinematic viscosity ν — typical ~1.0×10⁻⁴ m²/s for light machinery oil. Use this if nothing else is given; otherwise state your assumption.
  - Pipe roughness ε — nothing specified, so assume **smooth pipe**.

**Suggested procedure:**
1. Re = vD/ν = (1.5)(0.080) / (1.0×10⁻⁴) ≈ 1200 — transitional/low-turbulent range.
2. For a smooth pipe, use an appropriate correlation (e.g., Blasius for turbulent smooth pipe: f = 0.316·Re⁻⁰·²⁵, or the Moody chart). With Re ≈ 1200 and smooth wall, you get f ≈ 0.045 (Blasius is actually for Re > 4000 fully turbulent — at Re ≈ 1200 you'd be near laminar/transitional, so f from Moody chart is more honest; either way, **do not just use 0.03**).
3. Plug into Darcy–Weisbach: Δp = f · (25/0.080) · (880 · 1.5² / 2).

If you want a single number my default gives Δp ≈ 1.55×10⁴ Pa (≈ 15.5 kPa) using f≈0.045 from the Moody chart at Re≈1200, smooth pipe. But please state the assumed ρ and ν in your solution — they're not in the problem statement.
