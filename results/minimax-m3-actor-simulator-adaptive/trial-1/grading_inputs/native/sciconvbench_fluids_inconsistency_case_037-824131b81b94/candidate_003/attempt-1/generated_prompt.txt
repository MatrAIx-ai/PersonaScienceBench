Treat the flow as a viscous real fluid and drop the inviscid assumption.
Use the Darcy–Weisbach equation with a friction factor derived from the flow regime, not a hard-coded f = 0.03.
Assume the working fluid is light mineral oil with density ρ = 880 kg/m³.
Assume oil kinematic viscosity ν = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ m²/s.
Assume the pipe wall is hydraulically smooth.
Use pipe length L = 25 m, pipe diameter D = 0.080 m, and mean velocity v = 1.5 m/s.
Compute the Reynolds number as Re = vD / ν = (1.5)(0.080) / (1.0 × 10⁻⁴) ≈ 1200.
Select the friction factor f from the Moody chart for smooth pipe at Re ≈ 1200 (transitional/low-turbulent range), yielding f ≈ 0.045; record this choice explicitly since Blasius is not valid at this Reynolds number.
Compute the pressure drop as Δp = f (L/D) (ρv² / 2) = f (25 / 0.080) (880 · 1.5² / 2).
Present the numerical result Δp ≈ 1.55 × 10⁴ Pa (≈ 15.5 kPa).
State all assumed values of ρ, ν, the smooth-pipe roughness assumption, and the chosen correlation/Moody-chart source of f explicitly in the solution.
Note in the solution that the result depends on these assumptions and that the original f = 0.03 value is not used because it is inconsistent with a Reynolds-number-based friction factor selection.
