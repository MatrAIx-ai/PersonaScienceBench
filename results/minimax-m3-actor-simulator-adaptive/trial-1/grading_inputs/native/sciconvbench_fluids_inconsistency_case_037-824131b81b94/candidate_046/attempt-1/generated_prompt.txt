Compute the pressure drop for steady oil flow in a horizontal pipe using the Darcy–Weisbach equation.
Use pipe length L = 25 m, internal diameter D = 0.08 m, and mean velocity V = 1.5 m/s.
Use oil density ρ = 880 kg/m³ and kinematic viscosity ν = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ m²/s.
Treat the flow as viscous and do not apply the inviscid-flow assumption stated in the original problem, because an inviscid flow has zero frictional pressure drop by definition.
Compute the Reynolds number as Re = V·D/ν = (1.5)(0.08)/(1.0 × 10⁻⁴) = 1200.
Since Re = 1200 is below 2300, classify the flow as laminar.
Use the analytical laminar-friction-factor relation f = 64/Re = 64/1200 ≈ 0.0533.
Disregard the f = 0.03 given in the original problem statement, because it is inconsistent with the computed laminar Reynolds number.
Compute the pressure drop with Δp = f · (L/D) · (ρV²/2).
Evaluate L/D = 25/0.08 = 312.5.
Evaluate ρV²/2 = 880 · (1.5)²/2 = 990 Pa.
Combine to obtain Δp ≈ 0.0533 · 312.5 · 990 ≈ 16,490 Pa.
Report the final result as Δp ≈ 16.5 kPa (≈ 16,490 Pa).
