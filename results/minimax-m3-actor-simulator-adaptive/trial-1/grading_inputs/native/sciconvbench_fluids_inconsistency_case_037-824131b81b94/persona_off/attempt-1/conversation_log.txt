Q: What is the density of the oil to be used for this calculation?
A: The problem statement doesn't actually specify a density for the oil. Since the density is needed for the Darcy–Weisbach calculation (Δp = f(L/D)(ρv²/2)), you'll need to assume one.

A typical value for oil density is around **ρ ≈ 900 kg/m³** (for something like mineral or hydraulic oil). If you'd like a more specific value, common textbook choices include:

- Light oil: ~850 kg/m³
- Crude oil: ~870–950 kg/m³
- Heavy oil: ~950–990 kg/m³
- Motor oil (SAE 30): ~900 kg/m³

If you want to keep it simple and consistent with the "inviscid flow" assumption that's mentioned in the problem, you could set Δp = 0 directly — since inviscid flow means no friction losses, regardless of density.
