Use OpenFOAM to simulate a two-dimensional incompressible laminar lid-driven cavity flow using the transient solver icoFoam.

Build the geometry as a square cavity with length 0.1 m in the x-direction and height 0.1 m in the y-direction, extruded by a single cell layer of 0.01 m thickness in the z-direction to keep the case effectively two-dimensional.

Generate the mesh using blockMesh as a uniform Cartesian grid with 20 cells in x, 20 cells in y, and 1 cell in z, giving an overall mesh of 20 × 20 × 1 and a uniform cell spacing of 0.005 m in x and y and 0.01 m in z, with vertices located at (0 0 0), (0.1 0 0), (0.1 0.1 0), and (0 0.1 0).

Set the front and back patches in the z-direction to the empty boundary condition to enforce two-dimensionality.

Set the kinematic viscosity to 0.001 m²/s, which together with the lid velocity and cavity length gives a Reynolds number of Re = (1 × 0.1) / 0.001 = 100 based on U·L/ν.

Define the top wall patch as movingWall with a fixed velocity boundary condition of (1 0 0) m/s, and define the left, right, and bottom wall patches as fixedWalls with a no-slip (0 0 0) m/s fixed velocity boundary condition.

Apply appropriate zero-gradient boundary conditions for pressure on all walls.

Initialize the internal field with velocity (0 0 0) m/s and pressure 0 Pa, starting the flow from rest.

Configure controlDict with startTime 0, endTime 0.5 s, deltaT 0.002 s, writeControl timeStep, and writeInterval 20, so that 250 time steps are run and the flow field is written every 0.04 s, for a total of about 13 saved time directories.

Run icoFoam to advance the incompressible laminar Navier–Stokes equations in time using the PISO algorithm, producing the transient velocity and pressure evolution inside the cavity until t = 0.5 s at Re = 100.
