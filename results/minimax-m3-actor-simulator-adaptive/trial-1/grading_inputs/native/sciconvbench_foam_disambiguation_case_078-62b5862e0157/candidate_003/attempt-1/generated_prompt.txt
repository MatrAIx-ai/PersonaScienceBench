The simulation solves gravity-driven sloshing of water in a closed rectangular tank using the OpenFOAM solver interFoam.
The tank dimensions are 1.0 m in x (length), 0.6 m in y (height), and 0.1 m in z (depth).
The mesh is generated with blockMesh as 200 × 120 × 1 cells along x, y, z, creating an effectively 2D setup.
The patches front and back (z = 0 and z = 0.1) are set to type empty so OpenFOAM treats the case as a 2D plane-strain problem.
The boundary patches are leftWall, rightWall, bottom, top, front, and back; leftWall, rightWall, and bottom are stationary no-slip walls, and top is the open atmospheric boundary.
Gravity is set to (0 -9.81 0) m/s², directed along the negative y-axis.
The flow regime is laminar; constant/transportProperties defines two fluid phases (water and air) with their densities and kinematic viscosities, and RASProperties uses RASModel laminar with turbulence on.
The simulation is transient and gravity-driven, with no external excitation, moving walls, or imposed momentum sources.
Initial velocity U is uniform (0 0 0) everywhere.
Initial pressure p_rgh is uniform 0; hydrostatic equilibrium is established dynamically in the first steps because the interface is initially out of equilibrium.
The initial free surface is inclined linearly from a water height of 0.25 m at x = 0 to 0.35 m at x = 1, producing a mean depth of 0.30 m (half-filled) and a slope of about 5.7°.
The inclined interface is initialized with the standard setFields utility using setFieldsDict with ten boxToCell regions of width 0.1 m in x covering z fully (box from -1 to 1), where water height per slice follows the table 0.255, 0.265, 0.275, 0.285, 0.295, 0.305, 0.315, 0.325, 0.335, 0.345 m; this staircase approximation is smoothed by interfaceCompression within the first fractions of a second.
The 0/alpha.water field initially has internalField uniform 0 (air everywhere) and is modified by setFields after copying 0.org to 0.
Boundary conditions for alpha.water are zeroGradient on leftWall, rightWall, bottom, inletOutlet (inletValue 0) on top, and empty on front and back.
Boundary conditions for U are noSlip on leftWall, rightWall, bottom, pressureInletOutletVelocity with uniform (0 0 0) on top, and empty on front and back.
Boundary conditions for p_rgh are fixedFluxPressure uniform 0 on leftWall, rightWall, bottom, totalPressure with p0 uniform 0 on top, and empty on front and back.
Time controls in controlDict use application interFoam, startFrom startTime, startTime 0, stopAt endTime, endTime 10, deltaT 1e-3 as the starting value, writeControl timeStep, writeInterval 0.05, writeFormat binary, writePrecision 6, writeCompression off, timeFormat general, timePrecision 6, purgeWrite 0, runTimeModifiable yes, adjustTimeStep yes, maxCo 0.5, maxAlphaCo 0.5, and maxDeltaT 1.
The time step is therefore variable, governed by both maxCo and maxAlphaCo, allowing the solver to throttle at the start of motion when the interface is steep and to relax as motion settles.
Results are written every 0.05 s, yielding about 200 snapshots over the 10 s simulation.
The ddtSchemes default is Euler for first-order time accuracy and robustness around the initially sharp interface.
The gradSchemes default is Gauss linear.
The divSchemes use Gauss interfaceCompression vanLeer 1 for div(phi,alpha) with cAlpha equal to 1 to keep the free surface sharp; Gauss linearUpwind grad(U) for div(rhoPhi,U); Gauss linear for div(phirb,alpha); Gauss linearUpwind grad(U) for div(phi,U); Gauss linear for the viscous term div(((mu*dev2(T(grad(U)))))); and Gauss linearUpwind grad(k) and Gauss linearUpwind grad(omega) entries kept available for a future switch to a turbulence model.
The laplacianSchemes default is Gauss linear corrected, suitable for the orthogonal blockMesh grid.
The interpolationSchemes default is linear and the snGradSchemes default is corrected.
The intended execution sequence is blockMesh, checkMesh, cp -r 0.org 0, setFields, optional inspection of alpha.water in ParaView, and then interFoam.
The physical outcome expected is that the inclined free surface triggers sloshing from the hydrostatic pressure imbalance, the water sloshes back and forth in the rectangular tank, and the interface gradually settles or oscillates depending on viscous damping, all reproduced by the standard laminar interFoam setup without any additional forcing.
