Use OpenFOAM with the reactingParcelFoam solver to simulate a transient 2D axisymmetric spray combustion of liquid n-dodecane injected into a hot co-flowing air stream inside a cylindrical chamber.

Run the case in three-dimensional wedge geometry, but treat it as 2D axisymmetric by using wedge boundary conditions on the front and back planes (azimuthal span of 1°).

Build the geometry as a cylinder of length 0.2 m along the axial z-direction and radius 0.05 m in the radial r-direction, with the inlet at z = 0, the outlet at z = 0.2, the curved side wall at r = 0.05, and the axis at r = 0.

Generate the computational mesh with blockMesh as a structured 100 × 50 grid in the axial-z × radial-r directions, with grading that refines cells near the axis and near the inlet face so that the spray region is well resolved.

Apply k-epsilon RAS turbulent closure using the standard kEpsilon model with enabled wall functions in constant/RASProperties.

Apply the following boundary conditions. On the inlet at z = 0: fixedValue velocity U = (10 0 0) m/s, fixedValue temperature T = 800 K, inletOutlet total pressure p with p0 = 101325 Pa, fixedValue turbulent kinetic energy k with magnitude derived from 5% turbulence intensity and length scale of 0.005 m, and fixedValue epsilon derived from the same intensity and length scale using k^(3/2)/l. On this same inlet face, fix the gas composition to air by setting O2 mass fraction = 0.233 and N2 mass fraction = 0.767. On the outlet at z = 0.2: totalPressure with p0 = 101325 Pa, zeroGradient for U, T, k, epsilon, and all species mass fractions. On the curved side wall: noSlip fixedValue velocity U = 0, fixedValue T = 400 K, k lowRe wall treatment or standard wall function as resolved by the model, and epsilon consistent with the wall treatment. On the axis at r = 0: axis (or wedge) boundary type, where radial velocity, radial gradients, and tangential components vanish. On the wedge planes: wedge boundary type.

Initialise the chamber interior uniformly at time t = 0 with U = (0 0 0) m/s, T = 800 K, p = 101325 Pa, low-level turbulence k = 0.01 m²/s², epsilon = 0.1 m²/s³, O2 mass fraction = 0.233, N2 mass fraction = 0.767, and zero fuel (C12H26), CO, CO2, and H2O mass fractions.

Configure gas-phase combustion using the PaSR (Partially Stirred Reactor) combustion model in constant/combustionProperties with Cmix = 1.0, kappa = 1.0, turbulentReaction on, and semiImplicit no, so the chemical source terms are coupled to the turbulent mixing time scale tau_mix = k/epsilon.

Use a two-step global reaction mechanism in constant/reactionProperties with janaf thermodynamics, modelled as reaction 1: C12H26 + 12.5 O2 → 12 CO + 13 H2O, and reaction 2: CO + 0.5 O2 → CO2, with Arrhenius parameters typical of the Westbrook-Dryer fuel/CO substep finite-rate formulation (i.e., activation temperatures of the order of 15000 K and 4000 K, with pre-exponential factors roughly 1e9 and 1e8 respectively) and the temperature limits enforced by the turbulentReaction switch inside PaSR.

Set thermophysical properties of the gas mixture in constant/thermophysicalProperties using a multi-species reactingFoam-style thermoType with janaf polynomials, transportSutherland/hConst, and a mixture-averaged or default transport model for the species C12H26, O2, N2, CO, CO2, H2O with composition read from a chem.inp-style thermo file.

Enable the Lagrangian spray and define a liquid cloud named "sprayCloud" with thermoType set to liquid, density set to constant rho_liquid = 750 kg/m³ for n-dodecane, Cp set to roughly 2400 J/(kg·K), latent heat and saturation pressure following an Antoine-type correlation for dodecane, heat and mass transfer by the Ranz-Marshall model, drag by the standard sphere-drag model, no breakup (or standard breakup if needed to enrich fidelity), and no devolatilisation or surface combustion.

Inject droplets using a coneNozzleInjection model located at position (0 0 0) and directed along the unit vector (0 0 1) with thetaInner = 20° and thetaOuter = 20° forming a solid 20° half-angle cone centred on the inlet face. Specify a mass flow rate of 0.002 kg/s with an injection velocity U_mag = 25 m/s such that the implied effective orifice diameter is about 3.7 × 10⁻⁴ m, chosen for consistency with the chosen flow rate, density, and velocity.

Set the droplet temperature at injection to 300 K.

Distribute the droplet sizes according to a Rosin-Rammler distribution with minimum diameter d_min = 1 × 10⁻⁶ m, maximum diameter d_max = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ m, mean diameter d_mean = 50 × 10⁻⁶ m, and spread exponent n = 2.5 within the sprayCloudProperties distribution dictionary.

Set the parcel injection to inject on the inlet patch only, with massFlowRate injection, and use a parcel count of about 1000 parcels per timestep or per second consistent with typical reactingParcelFoam spray settings.

Do not enable any gas-phase radiation model; set radiationModel to none in constant/radiationProperties and do not include the G or Qr fields in the IO object list or in the energy equation source terms.

Run the simulation with adjustable time stepping, an initial deltaT on the order of 1 × 10⁻⁶ s, a maximum Courant number for the gas phase of about 0.5, a stricter spray Courant limit typically below 0.1, and an endTime of 0.05 s so that the case resolves the spray ignition and the early flame development.

Write numerical schemes in system/fvSchemes using Euler time derivative, Gauss linear gradients, Gauss linearUpwind for divergence of U, h, k, epsilon and species, Gauss linear corrected for Laplacian, and linear interpolation.

Configure linear solvers in system/fvSolution using GAMG for p with absolute tolerance 1 × 10⁻⁷, smoothSolver or PBiCG with absolute tolerance 1 × 10⁻⁷ for U, h, k, epsilon, and species, and SIMPLE or PIMPLE for pressure–velocity coupling as required by the chosen reactingParcelFoam configuration.

Save results every 1 × 10⁻³ s in writeFormat ASCII to provide about 50 output directories over the simulated time window.

Prepare post-processing so that droplet evaporation, vapor mixing, and combustion can be observed as a transient sequence showing droplet trajectories and evaporation along the chamber centreline, ignition of the fuel vapour near the inlet, formation of a non-premixed diffusion flame, and a thermal plume developing along the axis towards the outlet, with the side wall held at 400 K providing a cold boundary that quenches flame attachment near the wall.
