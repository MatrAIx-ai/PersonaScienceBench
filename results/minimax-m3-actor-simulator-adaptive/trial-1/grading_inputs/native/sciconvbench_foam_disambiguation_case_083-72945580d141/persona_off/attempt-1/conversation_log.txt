- **Velocity (`U`):** `zeroGradient` — velocity flows out passively
- **Temperature (`T`):** `zeroGradient` — temperature isn't fixed at the exit; whatever the local gas temperature is, that's what it carries out
- **Species (Y_i):** `zeroGradient` — species mass fractions flow out by advection/diffusion with zero normal gradient
- **Turbulence (`k`, `epsilon`):** `zeroGradient` — same treatment as velocity, the values at the outlet patch are extrapolated from the interior

**Why `totalPressure` and not `fixedValue`?**
At the outlet of a reacting flow, you typically don't know the exact static pressure in advance because combustion and heat release modify the local gas density and pressure fields. `totalPressure` accounts for the dynamic pressure contribution and is more stable for backflow-prevention scenarios. 1. **`p_rgh` vs `p`:** In `reactingParcelFoam`, you'll typically work with `p_rgh` (pressure minus hydrostatic `rho*g*h`). 2. **Inlet/outlet pairing:** The chamber is open at one end (outlet), which means the flow will develop naturally. With a 10 m/s inlet and a `totalPressure` outlet at atmospheric, you're essentially modeling a flow-through chamber, not a sealed one. Pressure waves and combustion-driven flow fluctuations should be able to exit cleanly.

3. **No backflow treatment needed for species/temperature at outlet:** Even if you want `inletOutlet`-style logic for `U` to allow potential backflow, the requirement specifies `zeroGradient` — so stick with that for simplicity. Q: To finalize the case setup, `controlDict` still needs the simulation duration.

What is the end time for the spray combustion simulation — and should it use a fixed time step or an adjustable run-time-selectable scheme (e.g., `adjustableRunTime` with a target maximum Courant number)?
A: Based on the full requirement, the time controls are locked in as follows:

**End time:** `0.05 s` — this is quite short, which makes sense for a quick spray ignition/flame development study. You won't see a fully developed steady-state flame at this duration, but you'll capture the spray injection, evaporation, ignition, and the initial flame establishment.

**Time stepping scheme:** `adjustableRunTime` with `maxCo = 0.7`. This is the right choice for a reacting spray case because:

- The Lagrangian droplets impose their own constraint based on parcel crossing times (the spray library handles this internally)
- The gas-phase reaction zones create rapid density/temperature gradients that benefit from sub-stepping
- A fixed `deltaT` would either waste compute (during early flight phase when flow is slow) or crash (once combustion kicks in and velocities spike)

**Write interval:** Every `0.001 s`, which gives you 50 output timesteps over the full run — plenty of resolution to see the flame structure develop.

**Here are the specific `controlDict` entries to set:**

```
application     reactingParcelFoam;

startFrom       startTime;
startTime       0;

stopAt          endTime;
endTime         0.05;

deltaT          1e-6;          // small initial timestep, will be adjusted

adjustTimeStep  yes;

maxCo           0.7;
maxDeltaT       1e-4;          // optional cap so it doesn't get too aggressive

writeControl    adjustableRunTime;
writeInterval   0.001;         // corresponds to the 1000 writes over 0.001s intervals

purgeWrite      0;

writeFormat     binary;        // saves disk space for transient runs
writePrecision  6;

writeCompression off;

timeFormat      general;
timePrecision   6;

runTimeModifiable true;
```

**A couple of practical notes for this setup:**

1. **`maxDeltaT` cap:** Even though `maxCo = 0.7` will naturally limit the timestep based on CFL, I recommend adding a `maxDeltaT` cap (around `1e-4 s`) because during ignition transients you can sometimes get unphysically large jumps if the velocity field spikes suddenly from combustion. This prevents the solver from taking erratic huge steps.

2. **`adjustTimeStep yes` paired with `writeControl adjustableRunTime`:** These work together — `adjustTimeStep` controls how `deltaT` is calculated each step, and `adjustableRunTime` ensures the writes happen at fixed *physical time* intervals (0.001 s) regardless of how the timestep is being adjusted. Don't use `writeControl timeStep` here, or your outputs won't be evenly spaced in time.

3. **Initial `deltaT = 1e-6`:** Just a starting value. Once the adaptive scheme kicks in, it gets overwritten. The first few timesteps with a low-Mach reacting solver can be touchy, so starting small is safe.

4. **`runTimeModifiable yes`:** Allows you to tweak `controlDict` settings on the fly via `mpirun -reconstruct`, which is invaluable for a transient case like this when you might want to extend the run or adjust write frequency without restarting from zero.

**Summary:** Run from `0` to `0.05 s`, adaptive timestep capped at `maxCo = 0.7` (with `maxDeltaT` ≈ `1e-4 s`), writing every `0.001 s`. That's it — let the solver figure out the timestep on its own.
