Q: Before I finalize, one question on the solver choice — there's a potential contradiction I want to resolve:

`pimpleFoam` is fundamentally a pressure-based solver designed for **incompressible or weakly compressible** flows. For a fully compressible, inviscid Mach 2 jet with shock cells and possible Mach disks, the conventional openfoam solvers are **density-based** and shock-capturing, such as `rhoCentralFoam` (Kurganov–Tadmor scheme, ideal for supersonic/inviscid) or `sonicFoam` (Pressure-Implicit with Splitting of Operators, transonic/supersonic).

The `waveTransmissive` outlet and fixedValue supersonic inlet BCs are also much more idiomatic with these density-based compressible solvers than with `pimpleFoam`.

Do you want me to keep `application pimpleFoam;` exactly as you wrote it (perhaps for compatibility with a custom build / template / downstream workflow), or should I switch the solver to `rhoCentralFoam` to match the physics of an inviscid Mach 2 jet?
A: Yeah good catch — go with `application rhoCentralFoam;` in `system/controlDict`. That's the right call.

A couple reasons:

1. **The physics wants a density-based solver.** A Mach 2 inviscid jet with shock cells / expansion fans / possible Mach disk is exactly the regime `rhoCentralFoam` was built for — Kurganov–Tadmor central scheme, naturally captures shocks without artificial viscosity, works directly with conservative variables (ρ, ρU, ρE). `pimpleFoam` is pressure-based and would need thermal-density coupling workarounds and won't capture shocks cleanly — you'd be fighting the solver the whole time.

2. **The BCs you listed are textbook `rhoCentralFoam` BCs.** `waveTransmissive` for `p`/`T`, `fixedValue` supersonic inflow for U/p/T, `slip` walls — that's basically the canonical setup straight out of the rhoCentralFoam tutorial cases (forwardStep, shockTube, etc.).

3. **`pimpleFoam` would force a thermo + turbulence stack it doesn't really need.** With pimpleLoam compressible you'd end up configuring `heRhoThermo` + PIMPLE sub-cycling + an extra pressure equation, and the cost/benefit vs just running `rhoCentralFoam` is bad.

So in `system/controlDict`:

```foam
application     rhoCentralFoam;

startFrom       startTime;
startTime       0;

stopAt          endTime;
endTime         5e-3;   // or whatever gets you a couple flow-throughs
deltaT          1e-7;

writeControl    timeStep;
writeInterval   200;
```

(Small timesteps — CFL will be brutal in this case, expect ~1e-7 to start, adaptive via `adjustTimeStep` + `maxCo` ~0.5 is recommended.)

Also one small but important reminder: with `rhoCentralFoam`, the field names in `0/` are **not** `U`, `p`, `T` — they're **conservative**:

- `U` → kept as `U` (the solver will compute rhoU internally from rho and U, but you still initialize the `U` field with velocity values, same fixedValue BC works)
- `p` → kept as `p`, and **libraries the solver uses internally `rho` from `p` and `T`** via the thermo. You initialize `p` directly.
- `T` → kept as `T`, initialized with temperature.
- The solver also needs `alphat` (turbulent thermal diffusivity) and `epsilon`/`k` if you add turbulence — for pure inviscid slip-wall laminar, set `alphat` to default 0 or just leave it as a placeholder uniform 0 field.

So don't change your `0/` field list — just swap the solver. Everything else in the spec stays.

Q: Before I finalize, one critical physical detail is missing — the **ambient conditions inside the larger receiving domain** (the gas the Mach 2 jet discharges *into*).

This matters because it determines whether the jet is **ideally expanded**, **overexpanded** (Mach disk + barrel shock cells), or **underexpanded** (expansion fans + diamond pattern) — i.e., it dictates the entire shock-cell structure you're trying to capture.

Please clarify: what pressure, temperature, and velocity fill the domain initially and at the far-field/outlet boundaries?

For reference, common choices for a canonical Mach 2 jet case are:

- **Option A — Quiescent ambient at the same p/T as the inlet** (p = 101325 Pa, T = 300 K, U = 0). Since the inlet total/stagnation conditions are higher than the ambient static conditions, this produces an **overexpanded** jet with a Mach disk and the classic "barrel + triple-point + Mach disk + slip line" structure. This is the textbook free-jet case most tutorials use.

- **Option B — Ambient matched to the inlet's *static* p/T and U = 0**, but with the inlet specified as *stagnation* conditions (would require recomputing p, T at the inlet to be higher than ambient). More involved.

- **Option C — Co-flow** (ambient moving at some small velocity in the +x direction). Less common for canonical supersonic jet studies.

I recommend **Option A** as the most physically meaningful and most commonly documented setup — it gives a rich, well-validated shock structure out of the box. Confirm Option A is fine, or tell me the specific ambient state you want.
A: Yeah, **go with Option A** — quiescent ambient at p = 101325 Pa, T = 300 K, U = 0.

Reasoning straight from what the spec already pins down:

- The inlet BCs fix `p = 101325 Pa` and `T = 300 K` as **static** conditions (alongside the Mach ~2 velocity). Those exact values are the only thing consistent with a "ambient at 1 atm, 300 K" interpretation, and the spec doesn't override them anywhere.
- `waveTransmissive` for `p`/`T` and `zeroGradient` for `U` at the far-field/outlet already implies a non-reflecting boundary over an undisturbed ambient — i.e., the domain interior should be at the same static state.
- So the initial fields in `0/` should mirror the inlet's static state, just without the velocity:
  - `U`: uniform `(0 0 0)`
  - `p`: uniform `101325`
  - `T`: uniform `300`
  - `alphat`: uniform `0` (inviscid — needed placeholder for `rhoCentralFoam` even if unused)

A note on the physics, since it's worth flagging:

Strictly speaking, with inlet static p = ambient static p = 101325 Pa and matching T = 300 K, the jet is **ideally expanded at the inlet plane** — there's no pressure mismatch driving a Mach disk / classic barrel-shock structure at the very exit. What you'll get is a Mach 2 free jet into a matched quiescent bath: expansion/Prandtl-Meyer fans at the nozzle lip (if the inlet is a sharp edge rather than a fully expanded exit plane), a thin free shear layer, weak oblique shocks / Mach waves from KH roller-up, and a gradually spreading plume. Rich flow field, but no Mach disk.

If a textbook overexpanded structure with a clear triple point + Mach disk is wanted, the canonical move is to raise the inlet static *p* above ambient (and/or drop the ambient *p*) so NPR > 1. But that's a parameter change outside what the spec lists — the requirement explicitly fixes `p = 101325 Pa` at the inlet and says nothing about a different ambient p, so I won't invent one.

So: **Option A, exactly as the spec implies**. Domain = matched quiescent air at 101325 Pa / 300 K, U = 0. Initial fields mirror that, far-field BCs enforce it. Inlet fixedValue forces the Mach 2 inflow on top. That's the case.
