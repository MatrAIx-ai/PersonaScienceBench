Simulate a high-speed compressible supersonic jet with inlet Mach number 2.0 discharging into a larger quiescent domain using OpenFOAM.
The application is rhoCentralFoam, set in system/controlDict, since this is a density-based shock-capturing solver suited to inviscid supersonic flow with shocks and expansion fans.
Treat air as a perfect gas.
Configure constant/thermophysicalProperties with type hePsiThermo, equationOfState idealGas (also acceptable: perfectGas), and transport sutherland.
Run the case as laminar and inviscid with no turbulence model, so no constant/turbulenceProperties dictionary is required.
Use a 2D mesh extruded one cell in the z direction with the frontAndBack patch type set to empty.
Document the flat inlet patch as representing the exit plane of a converging-diverging de Laval nozzle whose throat and exit are sized so that the flow reaches Mach 2 at the exit plane, even though no nozzle geometry is included in the mesh.
Prescribe supersonic inflow at the inlet using fixedValue boundary conditions with U equal to 686 0 0 metres per second, p equal to 101325 pascals, and T equal to 300 kelvin.
Initialize the entire interior and far-field of the domain with quiescent air at U uniform 0 0 0 metres per second, p uniform 101325 pascals, and T uniform 300 kelvin.
Initialize the alphat field as uniform 0 everywhere, required as a placeholder by rhoCentralFoam even though it is not used in this inviscid setup.
The solver derives rho and the energy field E internally from the initialized p and T through the thermodynamic library.
Apply waveTransmissive to p and T at the far-field or outlet boundaries so any outgoing disturbances leave the domain non-reflectively.
Apply zeroGradient to U at the far-field or outlet boundaries.
Apply slip to U, fixedFluxPressure to p (or zeroGradient consistent with the inviscid assumption), and zeroGradient to T at the solid walls, with the wall being a non-conducting slip surface.
At T equal to 300 K and gamma equal to 1.4 the speed of sound is approximately 347 metres per second, so U equal to 686 metres per second gives Mach number approximately 1.98, which is consistent with the stated Mach 2 inlet.
Start the simulation from time 0 in startTime mode.
Set endTime on the order of 5e-3 seconds so that several flow-throughs of the jet region are captured.
Use an initial deltaT on the order of 1e-7 seconds and enable adjustTimeStep with maxCo approximately 0.5 to maintain stability in the supersonic core where CFL numbers would otherwise be enormous.
Use writeControl timeStep and a writeInterval on the order of 200 so that the transient shock-cell and shear-layer evolution is captured.
With inlet static pressure equal to ambient static pressure of 101325 pascals and matched static temperature of 300 K, the jet is ideally expanded at the inlet plane, producing Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans at the inlet lip if a sharp-edge geometry is implied, a thin free shear layer along the plume boundary, weak oblique shocks or Mach waves radiated from Kelvin-Helmholtz roller-up in that shear layer, and a gradually spreading plume rather than a developed Mach disk with triple point.
If a textbook overexpanded free-jet structure with a clear Mach disk and triple point is desired in a future variant, raise the inlet static pressure above ambient or lower the ambient static pressure to achieve a nozzle pressure ratio greater than 1; this is a parameter variation beyond the present specification and is not applied here.
