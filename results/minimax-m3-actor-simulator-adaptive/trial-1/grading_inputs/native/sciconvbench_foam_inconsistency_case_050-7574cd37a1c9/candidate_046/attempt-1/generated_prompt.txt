Use rhoCentralFoam as the application set in system/controlDict, since this density-based solver with central schemes is specifically designed for high-speed compressible flows and resolves strong shocks far more reliably than the pressure-based pimpleFoam.
Build a 2D mesh and extrude it one cell in the z-direction, declaring frontAndBack planes as type empty so the case is treated as effectively two-dimensional.
Size the computational domain to roughly eighteen nozzle exit diameters (about 15 D downstream plus a 3 D upstream buffer) in the axial streamwise direction and roughly twelve diameters in the lateral direction centered on the jet axis, where D is the nozzle exit diameter.
Treat air as a perfect gas by configuring hePsiThermo in constant/thermophysicalProperties with equationOfState { perfectGas; } and transport { sutherland; }.
Adopt an inviscid treatment by selecting simulationType laminar in constant/turbulenceProperties, leaving turbulence fields unallocated, so that slip boundary conditions can be applied without conflict with a turbulence model.
At the nozzle inlet impose fixedValue boundary conditions on U = (686 0 0) m/s, T = 300 K, and p = 101325 Pa, giving an inlet Mach number of 2.0 based on a reference speed of sound of about sqrt(gamma*R*T) ≈ 347 m s^-1.
At the outlet and far-field boundaries impose waveTransmissive for p, waveTransmissive for T, and zeroGradient for U so that waves exit the domain cleanly without spurious reflections.
On the solid walls use the slip boundary condition for U and appropriate adiabatic or fixed thermal condition (waveTransmissive for T is also acceptable), enforcing the inviscid approximation.
Set the initial internal field consistent with the far-field conditions (U = 0, T = 300 K, p = 101325 Pa) so the supersonic inlet establishes the jet and its shock-cell structure from a quiescent ambient.
Choose shock-capturing divergence schemes in system/fvSchemes, such as limitedLinear 1.0, vanLeer, or MUSCL, for div(phi,U) and div(phi,e), paired with linear or limitedLinear for grad schemes, to preserve the sharpness of the Mach disks and diamond shock cells.
Select an appropriate time step to keep the Courant number below or near unity in shock regions (rhoCentralFoam tolerates higher local Courant numbers, but a CFL target around 1 is a safe starting point) and run the simulation long enough for several flow-through times (on the order of 10–20 D / U_inlet) to reach a stable, periodic shock-cell pattern.
Write results frequently enough to capture the transient build-up of the shock structure (e.g., a writeInterval of about 1e-5 s is a reasonable starting point) and extend the endTime until the shock cells are fully developed and any startup transients have convected out of the domain.
