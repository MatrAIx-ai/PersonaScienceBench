The simulation is a 2D natural convection benchmark in a square cavity (de Vahl Davis case).

Use the OpenFOAM solver buoyantBoussinesqPimpleFoam.

The cavity is filled with air, modeled under the Boussinesq approximation for buoyancy.

Gravity is specified as g = (0 -9.81 0) m/s².

The cavity geometry is a square with an aspect ratio of 1:1.

The temperature boundary conditions are: left wall fixedValue T = 300 K (hot), right wall fixedValue T = 290 K (cold), and top and bottom walls zeroGradient (adiabatic).

The velocity boundary condition on all four walls is noSlip.

The pressure boundary condition on all four walls is zeroGradient, with the exception required by the solver.

The fluid is initialized with U = (0 0 0) and T = 295 K.

The thermophysical properties are defined with a constant density assumption, a kinematic viscosity appropriate for air (nu = 1.5e-5 m²/s), a thermal diffusivity appropriate for air (alpha = 2.0e-5 m²/s), a thermal expansion coefficient beta = 1/Tref where Tref = 295 K, and Prandtl number Pr = 0.71.

The simulation runs until steady state is reached, verified by monitoring the residuals and the vertical velocity at the cavity mid-height.

The output is compared against the standard de Vahl Davis benchmark results for the average Nusselt numbers on the hot and cold walls and the stream function extrema.
