The thermal-mismatch strain in a thin aluminum film bonded to a ceramic substrate is estimated using the standard thin-film-on-thick-substrate model.

Use the given coefficients of thermal expansion directly: α_film = 23 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹ for the aluminum film and α_substrate = 8 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹ for the ceramic substrate.

Assume the substrate is much thicker than the film and therefore acts as an effectively rigid body whose in-plane dimensions are set solely by its own thermal expansion, while the film is biaxially constrained in-plane to match the substrate.

Treat the cooling as a temperature decrease of magnitude 180 K, with the temperature change expressed as ΔT = −180 K (final minus initial).

Define the elastic thermal-mismatch strain in the film as the difference between its constrained in-plane strain and its free thermal strain: ε_mismatch = (α_film − α_substrate) × ΔT.

Substitute the values to obtain ε_mismatch = (23 − 8) × 10⁻⁶ × (−180) = −2.7 × 10⁻³.

Report the magnitude as approximately 2.7 × 10⁻³, or equivalently 0.27 percent or 2700 microstrain, of in-plane biaxial mismatch strain in the film.

Interpret the sign physically as tensile mismatch strain, because the aluminum film has a much larger CTE than the ceramic substrate and therefore tries to contract more on cooling, but is held back by the substrate and ends up in tension.

State explicitly that this is an order-of-magnitude estimate that neglects any stress relaxation by film plasticity, cracking, or interfacial debonding, and that the biaxial, rigid-substrate assumption is standard for thin films with thicknesses much less than the substrate.
