Compute the electrical resistivity of a wire using the given measurements.
The wire has length L = 0.492 m and electrical resistance R = 11.705 Ω.
The wire has diameter d = 1.944 mm, which must be converted to meters as d = 1.944 × 10⁻³ m so that the final resistivity is expressed in Ω·m.
Compute the cross-sectional area using the formula exactly as specified by the user, A = πd², even though this yields four times the true geometric area.
Substitute the diameter in meters: A = π × (1.944 × 10⁻³)² m² ≈ 1.1872 × 10⁻⁵ m².
Apply the resistivity formula ρ = R·A/L, with R in Ω, A in m², and L in m, so that ρ is obtained directly in Ω·m.
Compute ρ = (11.705 Ω × 1.1872 × 10⁻⁵ m²) / 0.492 m ≈ 2.836 × 10⁻⁴ Ω·m.
State the numerical result with appropriate precision based on the given inputs, acknowledging that this value is approximately four times the true material resistivity because the A = πd² formula was used as specified.
