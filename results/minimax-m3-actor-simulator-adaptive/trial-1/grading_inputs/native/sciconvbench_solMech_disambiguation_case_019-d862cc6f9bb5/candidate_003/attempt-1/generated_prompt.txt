Specification for Statically Loaded Circular Shaft Under Multiple Torques.

Use linear-elastic, small-deformation, Saint-Venant torsion theory for a solid circular cross-section; plane sections remain plane and the cross-section remains circular and undistorted.

Model the shaft as 1D along its axis x, with the fixed support at x = 0 and the free end at x = L = 1200 mm; use the right-hand rule with +x directed from the fixed end toward the free end as the sign convention for positive torque.

Set the circular cross-section to diameter d = 30 mm (so radius r = d/2 = 15 mm); the polar moment of inertia is J = π d⁴ / 32 ≈ 7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴.

Take the material as AISI 1020 steel with shear modulus G ≈ 76,923.1 MPa (derived from E ≈ 200 GPa and ν ≈ 0.30); work throughout in consistent N–mm–MPa units.

Apply two concentrated torques to the shaft: T₁ = +200,000 N·mm applied at x = 1200 mm (free end, positive sense), and T₂ = −200,000 N·mm applied at x = 400 mm (negative sense).

Confirm global equilibrium by summing applied torques against the wall reaction: R(0) + T₁ + T₂ = 0 gives R(0) = 0, so the fixed end supplies no net reaction torque.

Use the method of sections with the right-hand-rule sign convention: cut at any location x and sum the external torques applied on the portion of the shaft from the cut to the free end to obtain the internal torque T(x).

Determine the internal torque distribution in each segment: in segment 1, 0 ≤ x ≤ 400 mm, T(x) = T₁ + T₂ = +200,000 + (−200,000) = 0 N·mm; in segment 2, 400 mm ≤ x ≤ 1200 mm, T(x) = T₁ = +200,000 N·mm.

Use the elastic torsion governing relation dφ/dx = T(x) / (G J) to derive the local rate of twist, integrating piecewise over the two segments.

Apply the kinematic boundary condition φ(0) = 0 at the fixed end; by continuity also impose matching twist at x = 400 mm so the angle of twist is continuous across the applied torque.

Integrate to obtain the angle of twist: in segment 1, 0 ≤ x ≤ 400 mm, φ(x) = 0 (since T = 0); in segment 2, 400 mm ≤ x ≤ 1200 mm, φ(x) = T₁ · (x − 400) / (G J).

Evaluate the end twist: φ(L) = φ(1200) = (200,000 N·mm)(800 mm) / [(76,923.1 MPa)(7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴)] ≈ 0.02615 rad ≈ 1.50°.

Use the elastic shear-stress relation for the linear-elastic torsion of a solid circular shaft, τ(r, x) = T(x)·r / J, valid for 0 ≤ r ≤ d/2.

Recognize that segment 1 carries zero internal torque and therefore zero shear stress everywhere on its cross-section; segment 2 carries a uniform internal torque and produces a linearly varying shear stress across the radius.

Compute the maximum shear stress in segment 2 at the outer surface r = c = d/2 = 15 mm: τ_max = T₁ · c / J = (200,000 N·mm)(15 mm) / (7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴) ≈ 37.7 MPa; the maximum shear stress in segment 1 is zero.

Report the three primary response quantities for the shaft: the internal torque diagram T(x) over 0 ≤ x ≤ 1200 mm, the shear stress distribution τ(r, x) for each segment, and the angle-of-twist distribution φ(x) with φ(0) = 0 and φ(1200) ≈ 1.50°.
