Specification for minimum shaft diameter from an angle-of-twist limit under pure torsion.

Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics, a solid circular cross-section of diameter d, a uniform torque, and ignore any transverse shear, axial load, bending, or stress concentrations.

Adopt the polar moment of inertia for a solid round shaft, J = π d⁴ / 32.

Use material properties for 6061-T6 aluminum with shear modulus G = 25,939.8 MPa in units of N/mm² throughout.

Define the design parameters as length L = 1500 mm, applied torque T = 1,000,000 N·mm, and allowable total angle of twist over the length φ_allow = 0.75°.

Convert the twist limit to radians using φ_allow = 0.75 × π / 180 ≈ 0.01308997 rad before substituting into any formula.

Apply the standard torsion-twist relation φ = T L / (J G).

Solve this equation for the required diameter, giving d⁴ = 32 T L / (π G φ_allow).

Substitute the numerical values to obtain d⁴ = (32 × 1,000,000 × 1500) / (π × 25,939.8 × 0.01308997) ≈ 4.50 × 10⁷ mm⁴.

Take the fourth root to get the minimum theoretical diameter d = (4.50 × 10⁷)^(1/4) ≈ 81.9 mm.

Round up to a sensible standard size and report the required design diameter d_min = 82 mm, noting that 85 mm is an acceptable conservative alternative if the next 5 mm coarse standard is preferred.

Note that with d_min = 82 mm, the resulting angle of twist is φ = T L / (J G) ≈ 0.748° over the 1500 mm length, which is within the 0.75° limit.
