The response corresponds to a static linear-elastic torsion problem of a solid circular shaft.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The shaft is solid, circular in cross-section, and subjected to a single pure torque T applied at the free end while the opposite end is fixed.

The stated "shear loading" refers exclusively to the torsional shear stress produced by T; no transverse shear force is acting on the shaft.

The material is aluminum 6061-T6 with a shear modulus G = 25939.8 MPa.

The shaft has length L = 1500 mm.

The applied torque is T = 1,000,000 N·mm.

The design constraint is the allowable angle of twist φ_allow = 0.75°, which must be converted to radians as φ_allow = 0.75 · π/180 ≈ 0.013090 rad before use.

Standard Saint-Venant and torsion assumptions apply: cross-sections remain plane and circular, warping is neglected, deformations are small, and the stress distribution is uniform along the shaft length.

The governing relation for the angle of twist is φ = TL / (J · G).

The polar moment of inertia for the solid circular cross-section is J = π d⁴ / 32.

The relevant response quantity to be determined is the minimum required shaft diameter d, obtained by solving the inequality φ ≤ φ_allow.

This yields d ≥ (32 · T · L / (π · G · φ_allow))^{1/4}, giving d_min ≈ 82 mm (rounded up to a standard size as appropriate).

State that the result satisfies only the angle-of-twist criterion; any additional strength (yield/critical stress) or stability checks must be verified separately if required.
