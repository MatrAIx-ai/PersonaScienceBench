This specification governs the design of the diameter of a solid circular shaft subjected to pure torsion, such that the angle of twist does not exceed an allowable limit.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics throughout.

Assume the shaft is made of a homogeneous, isotropic, linear-elastic material (6061-T6 aluminum) with a constant shear modulus G = 25,939.8 MPa, independent of temperature or strain rate.

Assume the shaft has a uniform solid circular cross-section along its entire length, so the polar moment of inertia J is constant.

Assume the length of the shaft over which the angle of twist is evaluated is constant at L = 1500 mm.

Assume a static or quasi-static torque is applied, with no dynamic amplification, fatigue, or stress-concentration effects to be considered.

Assume the end conditions produce pure torsion with no bending, no axial load, and no transverse shear force, so that the internal state is pure shear stress due to torque only.

Assume the allowable angle of twist is measured between the loaded end and the reaction end of the length L and must be converted from degrees to radians as φ_allow = 0.75° × π/180 ≈ 0.013090 rad prior to use in any formula.

Assume the applied torque is constant along the shaft at T = 1,000,000 N·mm.

Keep all units consistent in the N–mm–MPa system.

Use the governing relation for angle of twist in a prismatic circular shaft under pure torsion: φ = TL/(JG).

Use the polar moment of inertia of a solid circular cross-section: J = πd⁴/32.

Rearrange the governing relation to solve for the required shaft diameter: d = [32TL/(πGφ_allow)]^(1/4).
