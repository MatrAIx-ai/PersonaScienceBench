Q: Is the loading only a torque applied to the shaft ends (so "shear" refers to the resulting torsional shear stress), or is there also a transverse shear force acting on the shaft that must be carried in combination with the torque?
A: Only torque is applied — just the twisting moment T = 1,000,000 N·mm at the shaft ends. There is no transverse shear force involved. The word "shear" in the original prompt refers to the torsional shear stress τ = Tr/J that develops in the cross-section due to the applied torque.

So the problem is a pure torsion problem: use φ = TL/(JG) with J = πd⁴/32, solve for d given φ_allow = 0.75° over L = 1500 mm, with G = 25939.8 MPa and T = 1,000,000 N·mm.

Note: 0.75° must be converted to radians (≈ 0.01309 rad) before plugging into the formula, since the equation assumes consistent radian measure.

**Minimum diameter calculation:**
d = [32TL / (πGφ_allow)]^(1/4)

d = [32 × 1,000,000 × 1500 / (π × 25939.8 × 0.75 × π/180)]^(1/4)

Let me compute the denominator: π × 25939.8 × 0.01309 ≈ 1066.6
Numerator: 32 × 1,000,000 × 1500 = 4.8 × 10^10

Ratio: 4.8 × 10^10 / 1066.6 ≈ 4.503 × 10^7
d⁴ ≈ 4.503 × 10^7
d ≈ (4.503 × 10^7)^(1/4) ≈ 81.9 mm

So minimum required diameter d ≈ 82 mm (rounding up to be safe).

No additional assumptions needed beyond what's stated — small-deformation linear-elastic torsion, uniform shaft cross-section, fixed-free (or fixed-fixed) end conditions for the angle-of-twist relation to hold as stated.
