Q: Got it — loading geometry is irrelevant since M is prescribed directly. But your stated formula `σ = M·y/I` with **y positive upward** and a **sagging (positive) M = +4×10⁶ N·mm** would give a positive stress at the top fiber, which is physically incorrect (sagging produces compression on top, tension on bottom).

Which sign convention do you want me to lock into the spec — the standard one (σ = −M·y/I with y up, so top is compressive and bottom is tensile for sagging M), or do you want M entered as a negative number with the formula kept as σ = M·y/I?
A: Haan, standard convention pakad lo: **σ = −M·y / I**

- M = +4,000,000 N·mm (sagging — positive sign lagao mat, value positive hi rahega)
- y positive upward (jaise diya hai)
- Minus sign formula ka hissa hai, M ka nahi

Yeh convention physics ke saath match karega:
- y = 0 (neutral axis pe): σ = 0 MPa
- Top fiber (y = +h/2 = +60 mm): σ = (−M·y)/I → **negative** (compression) ✓
- Bottom fiber (y = −h/2 = −60 mm): σ = (−M·y)/I → **positive** (tension) ✓

Bas yahi rakhna. Ab I = b·h³/12 = 50·(120)³/12 mm⁴ nikaal ke teen points ke stresses with units (MPa mein) report karna. Sign convention pakka fix ho gaya.
