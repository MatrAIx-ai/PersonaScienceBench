Use small-deformation, linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic solid mechanics assumptions.
Apply Euler–Bernoulli pure-bending theory, since only an internal bending moment is prescribed at the section under consideration.
Use the elementary flexure formula with the standard sign convention σ = −M·y/I.
Take the internal bending moment as M = +4,000,000 N·mm (sagging, positive by convention).
Use a rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm.
Place the origin of the y-axis at the centroid (neutral axis) of the cross-section and take y positive upward.
Compute the second moment of area about the neutral axis as I = b·h³/12 = 50·(120)³/12 = 7,200,000 mm⁴.
Evaluate the bending stress σ at three fiber locations on a vertical line through the centroid, namely y = 0, y = +h/2 = +60 mm, and y = −h/2 = −60 mm.
At y = 0 report σ = 0 MPa.
At y = +60 mm report σ = −M·60/I = −33.33 MPa, indicating compression at the top fiber.
At y = −60 mm report σ = −M·(−60)/I = +33.33 MPa, indicating tension at the bottom fiber.
Report all stresses in MPa with explicit signs so that positive values denote tension and negative values denote compression.
Transverse shear stress, shear deformation, and stress concentrations are neglected in this analysis.
