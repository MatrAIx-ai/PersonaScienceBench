Assumptions: linear elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material; Euler-Bernoulli beam theory (plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis); pure bending at the section (no axial force, no transverse shear, no torsion); neutral axis coincides with the centroid of the rectangular cross-section; small deformations.

Section properties: width b = 50 mm, height h = 120 mm.

Moment of inertia about the neutral axis: I = b h^3 / 12 = (50)(120)^3 / 12 = 7.20 × 10^6 mm^4.

Distance from neutral axis to extreme fibers: c = h/2 = 60 mm.

Section modulus: S = I / c = 7.20 × 10^6 / 60 = 1.20 × 10^5 mm^3.

Governing relation: σ(y) = − M y / I, applied at this single cross-section, using the convention that the sagging moment M is positive and the y-coordinate is positive upward (so a positive M produces compression at positive y and tension at negative y).

Applied internal moment: M = +4.0 × 10^6 N·mm (sagging).

Bending stress at the neutral axis (y = 0): σ = 0 MPa (exact zero, as expected for a material point on the neutral fiber).

Bending stress at the top fiber (y = +60 mm): σ = − M c / I = − (4.0 × 10^6)(60) / (7.20 × 10^6) = −33.33 MPa (compression).

Bending stress at the bottom fiber (y = −60 mm): σ = + M c / I = + (4.0 × 10^6)(60) / (7.20 × 10^6) = +33.33 MPa (tension).

The stress distribution over the cross-section varies linearly through the depth, from −33.33 MPa at the top extreme fiber, through 0 at the neutral axis, to +33.33 MPa at the bottom extreme fiber.
