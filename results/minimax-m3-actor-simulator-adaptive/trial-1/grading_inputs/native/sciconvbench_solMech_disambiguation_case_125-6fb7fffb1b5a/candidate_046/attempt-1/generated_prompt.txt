The analysis treats a rectangular beam under uniaxial bending assuming small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm.
A single internal bending moment of magnitude M = 4,000,000 N·mm acts in a sagging configuration.
The bending occurs only about the principal centroidal axis perpendicular to the height h; no moment about the orthogonal axis is applied.
The neutral axis coincides with the centroidal axis parallel to the width.
The coordinate y is measured from the neutral axis, positive upward toward the top fiber.
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the deformed neutral axis (Euler–Bernoulli assumption).
The bending stress at any fiber is given by the flexure formula: σ(y) = −M·y / I.
The relevant second moment of area about the bending axis is I = b·h³ / 12 = 7,200,000 mm⁴.
The maximum bending stresses occur at the extreme fibers located at y = +60 mm and y = −60 mm.
At y = 0 (neutral axis), the bending stress is σ = 0 MPa.
At y = +60 mm (top fiber), the bending stress is σ = −33.33 MPa (compression).
At y = −60 mm (bottom fiber), the bending stress is σ = +33.33 MPa (tension).
The stress distribution varies linearly with y across the full depth of the cross-section.
The sign convention used is consistent with the standard sagging-moment convention in which the top fiber is compressed and the bottom fiber is in tension.
