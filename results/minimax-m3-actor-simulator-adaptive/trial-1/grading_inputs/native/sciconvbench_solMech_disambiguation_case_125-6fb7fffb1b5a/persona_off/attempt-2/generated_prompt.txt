Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic solid mechanics with Euler–Bernoulli beam kinematics, so plane sections remain plane and normal to the deformed neutral axis and shear deformation is neglected.
Assume pure bending at the cross-section of interest, with the internal bending moment treated as a known, constant scalar at that section.
Use a prismatic rectangular cross-section of width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm, both constant along the beam length.
Place the origin of the cross-sectional coordinate y at the centroid of the section, which coincides with the neutral axis for pure bending, and take positive y as directed vertically upward.
Apply a sagging internal bending moment M = 4,000,000 N·mm acting about the centroidal horizontal (z) axis at the cross-section under analysis.
Compute the second moment of area of the rectangular section about the neutral axis as I = b·h³ / 12 = (50)(120)³ / 12 = 7.20 × 10⁶ mm⁴.
Use the flexure formula σ(y) = −M·y / I as the governing relation for the normal bending stress at an arbitrary fiber located at coordinate y from the neutral axis, taking M as positive for sagging.
Evaluate the bending stress at the three requested fiber locations using this relation.
At y = 0 mm (neutral axis): σ(0) = 0 MPa, since the bending stress vanishes on the neutral axis.
At y = +h/2 = +60 mm (top fiber): σ(+60) = −(4,000,000)(60) / (7.20 × 10⁶) = −33.33 MPa, indicating compression.
At y = −h/2 = −60 mm (bottom fiber): σ(−60) = −(4,000,000)(−60) / (7.20 × 10⁶) = +33.33 MPa, indicating tension.
Report the stresses as numerical values in MPa, with the sign taken from the stated convention (negative for compression, positive for tension), and note that the magnitude |σ_max| = M·c / I = M / (b·h²/6) = 4,000,000 / 120,000 = 33.33 MPa, where c = h/2 = 60 mm and S = b·h²/6 = 1.20 × 10⁵ mm³ is the section modulus.
State explicitly that the result is independent of the actual external loading that produced M, since only the internal moment magnitude, sign, and axis direction enter the flexure formula, and that this treatment neglects transverse shear stress, axial stress, and any stress concentrations or local effects at the section.
