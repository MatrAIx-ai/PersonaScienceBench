Under Euler–Bernoulli theory for a cantilever with a tip point load, a constant moment P·L over the full span is not the exact internal moment distribution (the exact distribution is M(x) = P(L − x)), and enforcing the fixed-end condition v′(0) = 0 together with M ≡ P·L produces a free-end rotation of P·L²/(EI) — it cannot be zero. Likewise, setting the free-end rotation to zero is inconsistent with a cantilever that has a non-zero end moment from the tip load.

These three assumptions cannot all be satisfied at once, so the answer depends on which one you actually want to drop.

Which approach should I specify?
A: Hmm yeah you're right, sorry about that — those extra instructions were muddled. Ignore the "free‑end rotation = 0" and "constant moment P·L" parts. Just do the standard cantilever analysis:

**Use the exact internal moment distribution**, not a constant:
$$M(x) = P(L - x)$$

**Boundary conditions at the fixed end:**
- deflection: v(0) = 0
- slope: v′(0) = 0

**Integrate EI·v″(x) = M(x)** twice, apply those BCs, and then evaluate at x = L.

That gives the textbook formulas:
- slope at free end: θ(L) = P·L² / (2·E·I)
- deflection at free end: δ(L) = P·L³ / (3·E·I)

The free end is supposed to rotate and deflect — that's literally what we're computing, so obviously they're non‑zero. Don't pin them.

Plugging the numbers:
- E·I = 200000 × 1,200,000 = 2.4 × 10¹¹ N·mm²
- θ(L) = (2000 × 1800²) / (2 × 2.4 × 10¹¹) = **0.0135 rad ≈ 0.773°**
- δ(L) = (2000 × 1800³) / (3 × 2.4 × 10¹¹) = **16.2 mm** (downward)

So please go with that standard approach. The free­‑end slope and deflection are the unknowns — that's the whole point of the problem.
