Q: For the sinusoidal vertical traction on the hypotenuse, which spatial coordinate should drive the spatial variation of the traction amplitude?
A: The sinusoid varies with **x** — global x-coordinate, not y.

So the traction (applied in the vertical/y-direction on the hypotenuse `x + 2y = 1`) is:

**t_y(x) = −5×10³ · sin(πx)** Pa

A couple of things to keep in mind when implementing:

- The label "σ_yy(y)" in the spec is just notation for *which stress component* is applied — the actual spatial dependence is on `x`.
- Since `x` ranges from 0 to 1 along the hypotenuse, `sin(πx)` naturally satisfies zero traction at both corners (x=0 and x=1), which is what you want for a consistent boundary-value problem.
- Parameterize the hypotenuse with `(x, y) = (s, (1−s)/2)` for `s ∈ [0, 1]`, then plug `s` into the `sin(π·)` argument. The outward unit normal to `x + 2y = 1` is `n = (1, 2)/√5`, so the traction vector is `t = (0, t_y(x))` — no need to project anything since it's purely vertical.

That's it — `x` is the driver.
