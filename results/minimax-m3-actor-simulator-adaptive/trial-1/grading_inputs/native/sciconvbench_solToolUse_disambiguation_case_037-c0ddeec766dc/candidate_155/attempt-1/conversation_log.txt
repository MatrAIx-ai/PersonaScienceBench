Q: For the applied torque on the small arc near (1, 0.25), what point should be used as the center of the applied torque when computing r in the tangential traction formula t_θ = M/(2πr)? For example, should r be measured from the hole center (0.5, 0.25), giving a constant r = 0.5 m, or measured from some other reference point?
A: For the torque application, the center used in the traction formula should be **the center of curvature of the small loaded arc itself**, not the hole center.

Specifically:

- **Center of the small arc** ≈ **(0.98, 0.25)** — this is the point about which the torque M = 800 N·m is applied. The arc is a segment of a small circle of radius 0.02 m centered there, with the arc itself sitting on the right boundary (x = 1) of the plate, spanning symmetrically in y around 0.25.

- **r in t_θ = M/(2πr)** is the **arc radius r = 0.02 m** (constant over the arc), *not* the distance from the hole center (0.5, 0.25). So:
  t_θ = 800 / (2π · 0.02) ≈ 6366.2 N/m (line force per unit length; plane-stress unit thickness).

- **Direction of traction**: apply it in the *local tangent direction* of the small arc — i.e., perpendicular to the radius vector going from the arc's center (0.98, 0.25) to each point on the arc. Do **not** replace this with a fixed global x or y component. For points on the right boundary, this produces an essentially vertical traction (perpendicular to the right edge) since the small arc there is nearly flat, but it should still be computed from the local tangent to be consistent.

- **Hole boundary** (around (0.5, 0.25)): leave it traction-free unless an explicit pressure load is prescribed; the torque is only applied on that small 0.02 m arc on the right side.

So to summarize: use (0.98, 0.25) as the rotation center, r = 0.02 m as the constant radius, magnitude t_θ ≈ 6366 N/m, applied tangentially around that small arc on the right boundary.
