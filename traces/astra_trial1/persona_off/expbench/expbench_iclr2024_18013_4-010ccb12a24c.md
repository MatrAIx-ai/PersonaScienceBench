## Objective and current status
Design a controlled test of whether replacing EM alignment with MinED alignment improves BBH performance. This is a design only: no alignment implementation, training, inference, or benchmark evaluation has been executed. Whether MinED actually outperforms EM is unknown.

The available material specifies the comparison names, but not what is aligned, how either algorithm operates, where alignment enters the pipeline, or the model recipe. These omissions prevent a source-faithful executable protocol today. The design below deliberately conditions execution on resolving them rather than supplying an invented source procedure.

## 1. Mandatory pre-execution specification gate
Before collecting confirmatory outcomes, publish and freeze a protocol containing:

- **Objects and causal pathway:** identify both sides of each alignment, their representations and provenance, the alignment stage, and the consumer of its output. Document the pathway from criterion to changed artifact to BBH prediction. If alignment does not affect predictions or their generating process, this is not a meaningful downstream performance comparison.
- **Operational treatments:** give executable definitions of EM and MinED, including preprocessing, comparison units, edit operations and costs if applicable, matching constraints, tie-breaking, thresholds, and unmatched cases. Identify which settings are common and which are intrinsic to the treatment. Names alone are insufficient.
- **Pipeline:** specify model roles and checkpoints, tokenizer, data and splits, prompts and demonstrations, training objective and optimizer if relevant, stopping rules, decoding, software versions, and randomness sources. Establish that each criterion can be substituted at the same intervention point. Otherwise characterize the comparison as a package comparison, not an isolated criterion effect.
- **Evaluation manifest:** verify every BBH task/configuration, item identifier, version, inclusion decision, and task-family mapping. Audit the supplied '23 multiple-choice and 4 free-form' categorization; do not use it to exclude tasks. Freeze task-family/configuration weighting and scoring before evaluation.
- **Analysis and execution:** freeze the estimand, seeds, replication count, confidence procedure, superiority rule, failure/retry policy, resource limits, and diagnostics.

For each specification, mark its provenance as source-supported or investigator-proposed. If source definitions remain unavailable, an explicitly investigator-defined MinED-versus-EM experiment could be conducted after approval, but cannot establish the performance of the unspecified original proposed method. No illustrative operational algorithm is adopted in this design.

## 2. Treatments and controls
Run two otherwise identical pipelines: EM alignment and MinED alignment. Use identical base checkpoints, available input data, preprocessing outside treatment definitions, prompts, demonstrations, downstream hyperparameters, evaluation items, answer parsing, and scoring. Do not separately tune the methods on BBH outcomes.

- **If alignment affects training or distillation:** rerun all affected stages for each treatment. Pair initialization, input order, and other exogenous randomness by seed where possible. Match prescribed update schedules and non-treatment hyperparameters. If the method intrinsically changes available aligned examples or training signal, retain that difference and report it rather than silently forcing equal coverage. Log realized updates, token exposure, and compute.
- **If alignment affects inference:** use the same frozen model and evaluation inputs, changing only the defined alignment operation. Pair decoding randomness where meaningful; record any limitation to common-random-number coupling when computation paths diverge.
- **If alignment affects preprocessing or selection:** regenerate treatment-dependent artifacts from the same source pool and hold their downstream consumer fixed.

The primary contrast is the total effect of the criterion in the frozen common recipe, including intrinsic coverage changes. It is not a comparison of independently optimized methods or a claim of equal runtime. A separately declared equal-compute sensitivity comparison may be useful if resource consumption differs substantially.

**Leakage safeguards:** do not use held-out BBH reference answers or confirmatory performance to select alignments, hyperparameters, thresholds, parsers, or stopping rules. Use separate development material for debugging and resource pilots. Audit prompt demonstrations and alignment data against evaluation items, record overlap, and disclose unknown pretraining contamination. Any task-provided answer options are distinct from the hidden correct answer. Freeze scoring before unblinding treatment outcomes.

## 3. Benchmark scoring and primary estimand
Alignment EM is a treatment, not the benchmark scoring function. Both arms use the same verified, task-appropriate BBH correctness rules and identical answer extraction. Do not give MinED more permissive edit-distance answer credit. Count malformed or missing answers according to a common predefined policy; report their frequency.

Proposed primary endpoint: macro-average task-family accuracy on the verified fixed BBH suite. If the manifest distinguishes families and configurations, give each family equal weight and average its configuration accuracies with predeclared weights. Equal weights across configurations within each family are a proposed default, subject to approval at the gate. Do not let a family receive extra primary weight merely because it is split into more files. If no family/configuration distinction exists, average the verified tasks equally.

For arm a and independent paired run r, let A[a,r,t] denote task-family accuracy under those frozen weights. Define S[a,r] = (1/T) sum_t A[a,r,t], and D[r] = S[MinED,r] − S[EM,r]. The primary estimand is Δ = E_r[D[r]], the expected accuracy change over the frozen pipeline's declared randomness, conditional on the fixed checkpoints, recipe, evaluation items, and suite. Report it in percentage points. For a fully deterministic pipeline, the estimand reduces to its single fixed-suite difference.

Secondary outcomes: per-task/configuration differences, an item-weighted suite accuracy, verified multiple-choice/free-form subgroup summaries, malformed-answer rates, alignment coverage and ambiguity, unmatched rates, edit-cost distributions where defined, downstream signal/exposure, and wall-clock time, peak memory, tokens, and relevant compute charges. Task-level superiority claims require a separate multiplicity-controlled plan; otherwise these are descriptive diagnostics.

## 4. Replication, uncertainty, and decision rule
**Stochastic pipeline:** the replication unit is an independent paired pipeline run, not an individual BBH item. For training interventions this means independent paired retraining, not repeated evaluation of the same trained model. Independent seeds generate run pairs; within each pair, shared randomness improves precision. Evaluate every run on the same suite.

Proposed primary interval: a paired run-level t interval for the mean D, using the standard deviation of D across R independent pairs and R−1 degrees of freedom. Pair first, then compute the interval; do not treat the two arms as independent samples. Freeze this choice before confirmation. It assumes independent run pairs and an adequately behaved distribution of paired differences; a pilot should assess whether the planned replication supports that approximation. If it does not, predeclare a suitable alternative such as paired run-level bootstrap with adequate replication, or restrict claims to descriptive evidence. Very few runs do not support dependable tail inference.

Proposed superiority rule: MinED is supported as superior under the specified fixed-suite estimand if the lower endpoint of a two-sided 95% confidence interval for Δ is greater than zero. If the upper endpoint is below zero, the evidence favors EM. An interval spanning zero is inconclusive—not proof of equality or evidence that MinED cannot help. Report the estimate, interval, and run-level differences regardless of the decision. Separately report whether a predeclared practically meaningful gain is supported; statistical significance alone need not imply practical value.

**Item sampling versus runs:** the primary fixed-suite analysis does not bootstrap items or tasks. All supplied evaluation items are evaluated, and task resampling cannot establish generalization beyond BBH. If uncertainty for hypothetical new items from these same tasks is desired, report a separately labeled conditional analysis using paired item resampling within configurations and retaining both arms' outcomes together. This requires an explicit item-population assumption and cannot substitute for independent run replication or support broader task generalization.

**Deterministic case:** if the entire treatment-to-prediction pipeline is deterministic, execute one complete pair, plus targeted reproducibility checks if affordable. Report the exact observed fixed-suite difference; duplicate deterministic runs add no statistical information. A positive difference establishes only that MinED scored higher on those fixed items under that fixed recipe, not a stochastic or broader-population superiority claim. Deterministic decoding alone does not remove uncertainty from stochastic training.

## 5. Actionable replication and resource planning
Do not assume a budget. After the gate, use development-only pilot runs to measure per-arm runtime, memory, storage, data exposure, and paired variability. Include full representative training paths when training is the intervention; tiny parser tests cannot estimate training cost or seed variance.

Choose a smallest meaningful gain δ before the pilot. With pilot paired-difference standard deviation s_D and a selected power, use an approximate paired-test sample-size calculation, R ≈ [(z_0.975 + z_power) s_D / δ]^2, then check finite-sample implications and uncertainty in s_D. A confidence-width target is an alternative if practical gains cannot yet be specified. Compare the resulting R against measured pair cost and the available budget. Freeze R before confirmation, exclude tuning/pilot runs from confirmatory analysis, and do not stop when a favorable significance result appears.

If affordable replication is smaller than required, disclose the attainable precision and downgrade the exercise to limited or descriptive evidence rather than treating more BBH items as a remedy. Set a symmetric policy for infrastructure retries, model failures, and incomplete pairs; report failures without selectively dropping unfavorable runs. For fully deterministic pipelines, budget for one complete pair, validation checks, and diagnostics rather than artificial seed replication.

## 6. Validation and mechanism checks
Before the main evaluation, verify each resolved algorithm with hand-checked fixtures, including no-match cases, ties, duplicate candidates, and boundary cases appropriate to the representation. Test scoring on valid, malformed, and free-form outputs. Confirm that artifact/configuration diffs contain only intended treatment changes. Verify that disabling treatment-dependent changes yields equivalent pipelines where the specification predicts equivalence.

Log alignment yield, unmatched objects, ambiguity, relevant distance statistics, changed artifacts, and downstream exposure and costs. These checks help distinguish improved matching from greater coverage, altered training signal, or implementation errors; they do not themselves establish BBH improvement.

A matched-coverage analysis may be added as a secondary diagnostic using an outcome-independent, frozen matching or subsampling rule. It estimates a different conditional contrast and must not replace the full-treatment primary comparison. If matching is not meaningfully defined for the resolved objects, omit it and explain why.

## Deliverable and interpretation
Archive the approved specification, manifest and weights, software/artifact identifiers, paired outcomes, resource logs, and deviations. Report aggregate and per-task results with the predeclared uncertainty and decision rule. Conclusions apply only to the resolved pipeline and verified fixed suite. With today's missing definitions and no executed experiment, no empirical claim that MinED outperforms EM is justified.
