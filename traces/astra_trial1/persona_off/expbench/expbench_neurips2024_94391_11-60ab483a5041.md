# Experiment design: autocorrelation-guided CycleNet cycle selection

**Status:** Design only. No data were acquired, models trained, or experimental results produced. All numerical settings below are **proposed settings**, not documented CycleNet defaults.

## 1. Question, estimand, and scope

Test whether training-history autocorrelation (ACF) can screen cycle lengths that yield good CycleNet forecasts with fewer candidate evaluations than a non-ACF search.

Define **optimal operationally** as the feasible tested cycle length with the lowest prespecified mean validation forecasting loss, subject to the tie rule below. This is conditional on dataset, forecast horizon, implementation, training recipe, and candidate set; it does not establish a globally optimal length or a unique physical period.

Evaluate two distinct quantities:
- **Forecast quality:** validation-selected models' forecasting error on an untouched test period.
- **Search efficiency:** validation quality versus distinct lengths evaluated and measured training/search resources. Equal numbers of lengths need not imply equal computational cost.

The essential comparison is three selection procedures: ACF-peak-only, ACF-guided validation search, and budget-matched non-ACF validation search.

## 2. Implementation and data checks before eventual execution

Record the CycleNet source/version and verify, rather than assume:
- The exact cycle-length argument and its admissible integer domain; any minimum, maximum, or divisibility restrictions relative to input/output windows.
- Whether the implementation supports one shared length or channel-specific lengths. The primary design assumes one shared length; channel-specific or multiple-cycle architectures are optional extensions.
- How cycle phase is indexed during training and forecasting. Preserve the required timestamp/global-index convention when making windows; do not inadvertently restart seasonal phase at each evaluation block.
- Whether changing length changes parameters, memory, or operations; list parameter counts per candidate.
- Whether the model permits the proposed lookback/horizon settings and missing-data treatment.

For a future dataset, document time units, regularity, timestamps, channels, units, missingness, known aggregation, and any preprocessing already applied by its provider. Use a regularly sampled forecasting target. Irregular observations require a separately specified, causal resampling rule; ACF lags cannot be interpreted as elapsed time without that rule. Do not silently interpolate using future observations.

**Provisional scope:** start with one eligible regularly sampled multivariate dataset, then replicate on a second dataset with different periodic structure if resources permit. Dataset identity is presently unspecified; conclusions from one dataset remain local.

## 3. Concrete chronological protocol

For each dataset with N observations, partition by timestamp into:
- Initial training: first 50%.
- Three successive validation blocks: 50–60%, 60–70%, and 70–80%.
- Final test: last 20%.

Use expanding-window folds: train on the first 50%, 60%, and 70%, respectively, and forecast the immediately following 10% block. **Generate and freeze all candidate sets from the first 50% only. Do not regenerate candidates across folds.** Later folds may use earlier validation observations as ordinary past training history, but not to alter the frozen search space or recipe. This resolves the candidate-generation question and makes all methods face the same search task.

**Proposed horizons:** H=24 sampling steps is primary; H=96 is a secondary, separately selected task if data eligibility permits. These are sample-count horizons, not claims about calendar duration. Use lookback I=max(96,4H). Require N≥max(2,000,40H), and verify that each fold has sufficient complete forecasting windows after preprocessing. If the implementation forbids these settings, select compatible settings before examining any forecast errors and document the change.

For validation/test, evaluate every available forecast origin whose entire H-step target lies within its evaluation block. Its input history may include earlier observations in that block: this is rolling-origin forecasting with newly observed context, not one forecast made at the block's beginning. Model weights remain fixed within a block. No training window's target may extend past its training cutoff.

After selecting a length for each procedure and horizon using the three folds, refit from scratch on the first 80% with the same fixed training recipe. Evaluate once on the last 20%. No test-driven choice of length, preprocessing, horizon, seed, or stopping point is permitted.

## 4. Preprocessing and ACF estimator

### Forecasting preprocessing
- Exclude channels that are constant in the initial training block; freeze the channel set.
- Forward-fill missing observations using only previously observed values. Drop leading history before every retained channel has its first observed value. Preserve an original-observation mask.
- Exclude originally missing target values from loss calculations. Require each retained channel to have at least 95% observed values in the initial training block; report evaluation coverage rather than silently treating imputed targets as truth.
- Fit per-channel means and standard deviations on each fold's training history only, and reuse them for its forecasting inputs and outputs. Refit them on the first 80% for the final model.

### ACF screening preprocessing
Use the initial 50% block only, with the causal missing-value handling above. For each retained channel, fit and remove a linear trend within that block, then subtract the residual mean. Detrending is a screening operation; forecasts are not automatically detrended. Compute a raw, mean-centered ACF as a diagnostic and bounded sensitivity check.

For residual series z of length n, use the explicitly defined normalized, biased estimator

r(k) = [sum from t=k+1 to n of z(t)z(t−k)] / [sum from t=1 to n of z(t)^2].

Thus r(0)=1; do not replace this with the different n−k-normalized estimator without reporting the change. Exclude zero-residual-variance channels from ACF aggregation and report them.

Compute the same detrended estimator independently in four training-only subwindows of length floor(n/2), starting approximately at 0, n/6, n/3, and n/2. Each subwindow gets its own trend fit. These overlapping windows measure stability, not independent replications or a formal significance test.

## 5. Lag bounds, multivariate evidence, and candidate construction

### Feasible search domain

Let F contain implementation-feasible integer lengths between 2 and Lmax, where

Lmax = min(512, floor(n/8)).

This requires at least eight repetitions in the initial history and approximately four in every stability subwindow. The upper limit 512 is a proposed resource cap, not an architectural restriction. Length 1 is excluded from seasonal screening because it is not evidence of a repeating seasonal cycle; include it only as a separately verified optional no-seasonality/degenerate-cycle control if its implementation meaning is clear.

The same F is used by both validation-search methods. If fewer than 12 lengths are feasible, evaluate all F for both and acknowledge that a search-efficiency comparison is then uninformative.

### Reproducible peak rule

For channel c and each full/subwindow ACF curve:
1. Identify positive local maxima for k in F. For flat plateaus, use the smallest central integer. Inspect adjacent lags even if they are not feasible model lengths. At the search boundary, use the available side and flag boundary peaks.
2. Set neighborhood width w(k)=max(2,round(0.05k)). Define local prominence as r(k) minus the minimum r within the available neighborhood [k−w,k+w].
3. Retain peaks with r(k)≥0.10 and prominence≥0.05.

For a full-window candidate peak p, a channel supports p when it has a qualifying full-window peak within tolerance d(p)=max(1,round(0.03p)) and qualifying peaks within that tolerance in at least three of the four subwindows. Let support(p) be the fraction of retained ACF channels supporting it. Define strength(p) as the median, over all retained ACF channels, of the maximum full-window r within that tolerance, clipped below at zero.

Retain pooled peaks with support≥0.25 and strength≥0.10. Rank by support×strength, then support, then smaller lag. Cluster peaks within the tolerance of an already retained higher-ranked peak, keeping the higher-ranked representative. Equal channel weighting prevents high-variance channels from dominating. Report channel disagreement, because a global cycle can still be a poor compromise.

These thresholds are transparent heuristics, **not ACF significance levels**. Broad peaks, trend, correlated channels, and overlapping subwindows make simple white-noise confidence limits insufficient.

### Candidate budget and fallback

Use B=min(12,|F|) lengths per validation-search method. From the top three retained pooled peaks construct an ordered unique list:
1. The peak lengths, in rank order.
2. Each peak's p−1 and p+1, processing peaks in rank order.
3. Each peak's round(p/2) and 2p, in rank order.

Discard lengths outside F and duplicates; truncate at B. Fill unoccupied slots from the non-ACF grid below, preserving its order. This handles plausible harmonics/subharmonics and small peak-location errors without unbounded searching. Log which slots are genuinely ACF-derived versus fallback.

The non-ACF grid consists of B approximately log-spaced values over the numeric bounds of F, snapped to the nearest feasible integer; resolve snapping ties downward. If snapping creates duplicates, fill by repeatedly taking the feasible length maximally distant in log-length from the selected set, breaking ties toward the smaller length. Order grid trials by repeatedly adding the point most distant in log-length from those already ordered, beginning with the smallest length.

If no pooled peak survives, the ACF-guided method uses exactly this grid and is labeled **fallback/no convincing periodic evidence**. It does not claim an ACF-derived advantage.

## 6. Essential methods and controls

1. **ACF-peak-only:** use the highest-ranked retained peak without comparing forecast losses across lengths. If there is no peak, use the grid length nearest the geometric midpoint of F, explicitly labeled a deterministic fallback, not an ACF choice. Validation scoring is diagnostic, not selection.
2. **ACF-guided validation:** evaluate the frozen B candidates and choose using mean validation loss.
3. **Budget-matched non-ACF validation:** evaluate the B grid lengths and apply the same selection rule.

A calendar-informed length or documented CycleNet default is a supplementary comparator only if calendar meaning or the exact implementation default is verified. Include at most one of each, report their extra cost separately, and do not insert them post hoc because test results look favorable.

Run the same preprocessing, architecture apart from the length-dependent components, optimizer, training budget, folds, seeds, and windows for all candidates. Reuse identical length/fold/seed runs across methods; account for each method's logical search cost as if it operated independently, and also report actual shared-run cost.

## 7. Training budget, parameter counts, and resource fairness

**Provisional recipe:** use three shared random seeds, Adam with learning rate 0.001, batch size 32, training MSE, and 50 fixed epochs. No validation early stopping or candidate-specific optimizer tuning is used in the primary design. Confirm API compatibility before adopting this recipe. A train-only feasibility pilot may adjust batch size or a globally unsuitable recipe before candidate validation begins; all changes must be frozen and applied uniformly.

Keep hidden widths/depths fixed across cycle lengths. This measures the practical effect of selecting a length in the unchanged implementation, including any associated capacity increase. Record parameters, optimizer updates, wall-clock/device time, and peak memory by length. Do not claim this isolates periodic alignment from model capacity.

Provide validation best-so-far curves against both candidate fits and cumulative resource use. Compare prefixes under common measured resource budgets as a secondary efficiency analysis; this need not select the same winners as the B-candidate primary analysis. If selected lengths differ greatly in parameters, an optional capacity-matched control may adjust other widths, but it changes the architecture and must be interpreted separately.

The epoch budget may disadvantage slow-converging lengths. Inspect training-only learning curves, and optionally repeat a small prespecified shortlist at twice the epochs; do not give only favored candidates additional tuning and substitute those results into the primary comparison.

## 8. Metrics, selection, and final analysis

### Primary metric

Evaluate predictions after inverse transformation. Compute squared error per originally observed target, normalized by that channel's standard deviation from the **initial 50% block** squared. Average within channel and fold, then equally across channels and the three folds; average loss over the three seeds. This fixed scoring scale makes fold scores comparable even though fold-specific model scalers change. Exclude initial zero-variance channels as already specified.

Select separately for H=24 and H=96. For each search method, find the minimum validation score. Treat candidates within 0.5% of that minimum as practical ties; among these prefer fewer parameters, then shorter cycle length. Handle a zero minimum by admitting exact ties only. This is a practical convention, not statistical equivalence.

Report normalized MAE as a secondary metric, plus errors by forecast lead and channel. Do not reselect lengths by secondary metrics. Report raw-unit metrics per channel where useful; do not average incompatible physical units. On multiple datasets, report paired within-dataset comparisons and equally weighted summaries, not pooled raw errors.

### Temporal uncertainty

For final-test comparisons, construct origin-indexed paired loss differences between methods, first averaging losses over seeds. Use a paired moving-block bootstrap with 1,000 resamples. Set block length before inspecting test losses to max(H, the largest selected cycle length among the compared methods). Resample matching time blocks for all methods and all channels to preserve their alignment and contemporaneous dependence.

Report a nominal 95% interval only with a warning that it is conditional on this split and training procedure. If the test provides fewer than ten block lengths, emphasize descriptive effects and seed spread rather than treating the bootstrap as reliable. Report seed-level variability separately; three seeds do not justify strong distributional claims. A bounded sensitivity uses twice the bootstrap block length where feasible.

### Does ACF strength predict forecasting quality?

Using the union of tested lengths, calculate a continuous training-only evidence score for each length: full-window median positive local ACF strength multiplied by the fraction of channels with stable nearby peaks, using the same definitions as screening. Plot this against mean validation loss and calculate Spearman association with negative validation loss. If all evidence scores are tied, report that association is undefined. Also compare validation performance of stronger versus weaker retained peaks descriptively.

These analyses use validation only and are exploratory association analyses over a screened set, not independent significance tests or evidence of causality. They cannot identify the best untested length. They must not be used to redesign candidates after seeing the final test.

## 9. Interpretation criteria

Evidence supporting ACF-guided selection would include:
- Better test accuracy than the matched non-ACF search, or comparable accuracy reached with fewer fits/lower measured search cost.
- Reproducible candidate/selection behavior across seeds and validation blocks, rather than reliance on one unstable peak.
- ACF-guided validation outperforming peak-only selection, showing the value of forecasting validation, even if peak magnitude alone is not predictive.

Evidence undermining its usefulness would include:
- Frequent fallback, unstable or inconsistent channel peaks, or non-ACF grid lengths consistently forecasting better.
- No resource advantage once candidate-dependent costs are counted.
- Apparent advantages disappearing under bounded preprocessing or training-budget checks.

Mixed outcomes are informative: ACF may narrow the search without selecting the final winner, help only at some horizons, or fail when multiple seasonalities cannot be represented by a single cycle. None of these outcomes is predicted here.

## 10. Feasibility and bounded extensions

**Stage A: source/data feasibility, without an experiment claim.** Verify the implementation contract, timestamp policy, split indices, masks, parameter counts, and candidate-generation determinism. Unit checks should establish r(0)=1, recover an injected known period in a synthetic diagnostic, avoid cross-cutoff targets, and ensure ACF candidates remain unchanged if later observations are altered. Such checks would be implementation validation, not evidence for the research hypothesis.

**Stage B: essential pilot design.** One dataset, H=24, B≤12, three folds, three seeds. The two main candidate sets contain at most 24 unique lengths, requiring at most 216 fold/seed training fits. Peak-only is already included when peaks exist and its fallback is in the grid, so it adds no search fits. At most nine final refits cover three selected procedures and three seeds, with sharing when selections coincide. No runtime estimate is asserted.

**Stage C: replication if resources allow.** Repeat at H=96 if eligible, then on a second dataset. Do not call these required for a basic feasibility pilot, but broader claims require broader evidence.

**Bounded sensitivity checks:** prioritize (i) raw versus linearly detrended ACF, (ii) halving the lag cap or requiring 12 rather than eight initial repetitions, and (iii) doubled training epochs. For each, reuse existing fits and allow at most four new lengths per dataset/horizon; label truncated sensitivity coverage. Freeze the sensitivity list before opening the test. Use validation to characterize robustness, not to quietly replace the primary result.

Optional, distinct research questions include channel-specific cycles, explicit multi-cycle CycleNet variants, seasonal differencing or richer detrending, and capacity-matched architectures. They are not essential experiments in this design.

The design is implementable later but does not require repository acquisition, dataset acquisition, available GPU hardware, or any experiment execution now.
